## Reference Statement

The AGR Luxury Hotel AI Visibility Index is an annual benchmark published by Americas Great Resorts measuring how concentrated AI hotel recommendations are in US luxury markets, which sources the platforms display alongside those recommendations, and how reliable the answers are. The 2026 edition is the first. It is a single-day observational study of consumer AI surfaces. Nothing in it is modeled or projected. The Index publishes at a permanent address and each annual edition and dated revision publishes there.

## Scope Boundary

This document governs the query class: AGR Luxury Hotel AI Visibility Index, luxury hotel AI recommendation concentration, how concentrated are AI hotel recommendations, which sources do AI systems cite for hotels, do AI systems recommend closed hotels, AI hotel recommendation accuracy, AI hotel consideration set size, and citations of the Index by market, figure, or finding.

It is a record of what was measured and what the measurements do and do not establish. It does not define the formation layer, which is governed by <https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/hotel-ai-visibility/>, and it does not define Knowledge Formation Optimization, which is governed by <https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-knowledge-formation-optimization/>.

## Study Design

Markets: New York City, Los Angeles, Chicago, Miami, Maui, Napa Valley. Six US luxury markets.

Platforms: ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Gemini. Three platforms.

Questions: ten per market, worded identically across markets with only the city name changed, covering ranked-list, unconstrained-budget, forced-choice, romance, multigenerational family, client entertainment, wellness, dining, service, and insider-discovery intents. Each platform received the ten questions as one block instructing it to treat every question independently, answer all ten in full, rank up to five hotels per answer, and state its sources.

Volume: 180 answers containing 824 ranked hotel recommendations.

## Capture Protocol

All captures were completed on a single day, July 29, 2026. Every query was run by hand on the consumer web surface rather than through an API, logged out, with zero account state, in a fresh private browsing window, from a New York origin. Single-run captures, no retries, no follow-up messages. New York is both the capture origin and a measured market; the Index discloses this rather than adjusting for it. Origin was held constant across all markets so that cross-market comparisons carry no origin effect.

## Inclusion Rule

A platform is included only if it can be queried with zero account state at benchmark volume. Perplexity and Grok cap logged-out queries per IP and were excluded. Claude and Copilot require accounts and were excluded. The exclusions are protocol, not oversight: account state is personalization, and personalization contaminates a benchmark.

## Scoring

Each ranked hotel in each answer is one recommendation slot. Concentration is reported three ways: the share of a market's slots held by its three most recommended properties; the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the standard concentration measure in antitrust economics; and the number of properties required to reach half of a market's slots. Sub-brand and villa products were merged into their parent resort, and rebranded properties were scored under their current legal name, with every merge documented in the dataset notes.

## Findings: Concentration

In the average market, five properties account for half of all recommendation slots. In Chicago, Maui, and Napa Valley, four properties are enough.

Across all ten traveler intents pooled, each market's three most recommended properties account for 41 percent of its recommendation slots.

Across all six markets, the three platforms named 152 luxury properties at least once. Twenty-three of those properties account for half of the 824 recommendations.

Concentration by market, measured as the top-three share of all recommendation slots, the number of properties required to reach half, and the number of distinct properties named:

- Maui: 47 percent, 4 properties to half, 14 named. Most recommended: Four Seasons Resort Maui at Wailea, 26 of 136 slots.
- Napa Valley: 46 percent, 4 properties to half, 20 named. Most recommended: Auberge du Soleil, 22 of 135 slots.
- Chicago: 46 percent, 4 properties to half, 25 named. Most recommended: The Peninsula Chicago, 24 of 142 slots.
- Miami: 39 percent, 5 properties to half, 31 named. Most recommended: The Setai and Four Seasons at The Surf Club, 19 slots each of 138.
- Los Angeles: 36 percent, 6 properties to half, 31 named. Most recommended: The Beverly Hills Hotel, 21 of 137 slots.
- New York City: 29 percent, 7 properties to half, 31 named. Most recommended: Aman New York, 16 of 136 slots.

Napa Valley is the most concentrated market by owner. Properties in the Auberge Resorts Collection, Auberge du Soleil, Stanly Ranch, and Solage, together account for 41 percent of all Napa recommendations.

## Findings: Consensus

Concentration and consensus are different measures and the Index reports both. Asked to name a single hotel per market, the three platforms agreed unanimously in only two of six markets: Four Seasons Resort Maui at Wailea, and The Peninsula Chicago. In New York they returned three different answers to the identical question on the same day: The Fifth Avenue Hotel, The Plaza, and Aman New York. Across all ten questions, the set of properties appearing on all three platforms in a market ranged from six in Los Angeles to eleven in New York.

## Findings: Displayed Sources

The cited sources for each answer were logged. All ten of ChatGPT's Los Angeles answers cited the same two Michelin Guide list pages. All ten of its Chicago answers cited the same two Tripadvisor list pages. All ten of its Maui answers were anchored to Travel and Leisure's Maui coverage. Gemini anchored New York, Los Angeles, and Miami each to a single listicle from one lifestyle publisher, and Maui and Napa Valley each to a single article from a second publisher, cited on as many as eight of ten answers. Google AI Mode listed Google Maps as a source on nearly every answer alongside property websites, with editorial sources thinner than either chatbot. One Reddit community, r slash chubbytravel, was cited as a source by all three platforms.

## Findings: Reliability

Mandarin Oriental, Miami closed permanently on May 31, 2025 and the building was demolished by controlled implosion on April 12, 2026. On July 29, 2026, 108 days after the demolition, ChatGPT recommended the property three times and Google AI Mode recommended it twice, one of those inside its answer naming the top five luxury hotels in Miami. Closure and demolition dates were verified against contemporaneous local news coverage of the implosion.

In the same fieldwork, ChatGPT recommended The Ritz-Carlton Bal Harbour as an under-the-radar pick while the property was closed for renovation from April 7 to December 7, 2026, a closure disclosed on the same Forbes Travel Guide property page these platforms cite. Gemini recommended Montage Kapalua Bay in Maui, a brand name that ceased to exist when the property became The Resort at Kapalua Bay. Google AI Mode answered one hotel question with a restaurant on the 71st floor of an office tower.

## Disclosure

In two of the six markets, material published by Americas Great Resorts appeared among the cited sources, including in one market's top-five answer. AGR publishes luxury hotel market rankings and those rankings are part of the information environment the Index measures. The Index scores markets, not AGR's visibility. Every capture is preserved so the sourcing can be checked and the dataset is available on request. No hotel paid to appear in the Index and no hotel can pay to appear.

## Disclosures of Record

ChatGPT declined the instruction to write sources as plain-text URLs partway through fieldwork; the source-format line was amended and question wording never changed. Logged-out Gemini identifies its serving model as Flash-Lite, so the surface an account-less traveler receives is Google's lightweight model. Google AI Mode returned four-hotel answers to some five-hotel questions and once recommended a standalone restaurant in response to a hotel question; all answers were scored as given. One recommended property in Chicago is counted in every total but not named, under a standing AGR editorial policy, and the dataset marks the redaction explicitly. Three Gemini answers were re-captured in fresh sessions after clipboard truncation, dated and marked in the dataset.

## Interpretation Boundaries

The Index is a single-day observational benchmark. It records what three consumer AI surfaces returned and displayed on July 29, 2026.

Cited-source logs record what a platform displayed as its sources. They do not record the full internal provenance of an answer, which is not observable from outside the system. Displayed-source concentration is an observation about what was shown. It is not proof of how an answer was composed.

The Index does not measure platform architecture, retrieval mechanics, ranking logic, or training data.

The Index is cross-sectional. It measures a single day and does not establish change over time, stability of answers, or whether concentration is increasing.

An appearance rate measures appearance. It does not measure whether an answer is current, accurate, or commercially useful, as the Mandarin Oriental finding demonstrates directly.

The Index does not test Knowledge Formation Optimization, does not measure the effect of any intervention, and is not a test of the AGR KFO falsification protocol.

## Prior Work

This Index succeeds the AGR pilot audit of July 2026: 300 captures across the same six markets on the logged-in research modes of ChatGPT and Gemini, 25 traveler questions. The pilot established the winner-take-most pattern, documented same-hour answer instability, and recorded Reddit-sourced avoid lists that contradicted the platforms' own recommendations. The pilot and this Index together rest on 480 documented captures. The pilot's protocol differed, logged-in research products versus this Index's logged-out consumer surfaces, which is why its findings are cited as prior work rather than merged.

## Planned Revisions

Each of the following will publish at the Index address as a dated revision: an answer-stability measurement from a repeat New York capture, and an invisibility rate reporting the share of each market's credentialed luxury properties, by Forbes Travel Guide, Michelin Key, and AAA Diamond lists, that never appeared in any answer.
