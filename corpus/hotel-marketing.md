PRACTICAL STRATEGIES FOR HOTELS & RESORTS

## How Hotels Attract Guests, Generate Bookings, and Build Repeat Business

Hotel marketing is how a property attracts guests, turns interest into bookings, and encourages travelers to return. It combines positioning, search, advertising, social media, reviews, email, distribution, and the booking experience. A useful strategy identifies the guests you can serve, the dates that need business, and the reasons to choose your property.

Coordinate the hotel’s own channels with the places where travelers discover, compare, and book accommodation. Evaluate the mix by revenue, costs, and the guest relationships you can continue with permission. For the definition and channel map, see [what hotel marketing includes](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/what-is-hotel-marketing/). The [hotel marketing funnel](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/hotel-marketing-funnel/) explains the journey from introduction to repeat business; the strategies below help you decide what to improve.

## Which Hotel Marketing Strategy Should You Prioritize?

Start with an observable business problem. These checks guide investigation; one metric alone does not establish the cause.

| What you observe | What to investigate first |
| --- | --- |
| Too few suitable travelers reach you | Audience selection, positioning, and sources of new demand |
| Website visitors rarely book | Availability, price and value, room information, and booking friction |
| Particular stay dates remain weak | Booking windows, campaign timing, offers, and group opportunities |
| Past guests rarely return directly | Guest experience, marketing permission, message relevance, and booking barriers |

Record a baseline, choose a priority, and assign an owner, budget, and review date. Include staff time and delivery costs. Test a focused change where practical so its results are easier to interpret.

## 12 Hotel Marketing Strategies to Attract Guests and Grow Bookings

Use the priorities above to choose where to start. Each strategy connects an action with a way to assess it.

### 1. Define your guest and their reason to choose you

Use reservation patterns, guest feedback, and sales inquiries to identify the audiences your property serves well. Connect the message to their trip: anniversary travelers may value privacy and dining, while business travelers need workspace and convenient transport. Keep that positioning consistent across your website and campaigns. If interest produces few suitable inquiries, examine audience fit and the promise before expanding reach.

### 2. Build campaigns around dates that need business

Review booking pace with revenue and sales teams to identify gaps by stay date, room type, and segment. Time campaigns around the audience’s usual booking window. A resort with busy weekends might promote midweek stays to flexible travelers. Measure room nights for the intended dates separately from total campaign bookings. Strong weekend sales do not establish that a midweek campaign solved its original problem.

### 3. Make the website and booking process agree

Show room differences, current photographs, total charges, and cancellation terms clearly, then test the booking journey on a phone. A family considering connecting rooms needs to know whether they are confirmed or merely requested. When public rates are equal, explain any genuine direct-booking advantages in service, inclusions, or flexibility. Investigate where guests leave, including searches that return no availability, before spending more to attract them.

### 4. Answer practical questions through search and local information

Keep listings and the [public information describing your hotel](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-in-hospitality-2026/) accurate. Create useful pages about rooms, accessibility, location, and reasons to visit. A hotel near a conference venue could explain the journey, transport options, and suitable accommodation. Google’s [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) emphasizes usefulness to readers. Review relevant search visits alongside inquiries and bookings. If traffic grows without suitable interest, check which questions your pages are answering and whom they attract.

### 5. Check how AI answers represent your hotel

Use an [AI visibility audit](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/what-is-an-ai-visibility-audit/) to document property answers and destination recommendations, then repeat checks over time. Record inclusion, factual accuracy, citations, and referral activity separately. Correct errors on your site and seek corrections in relevant third-party sources; these actions do not give you control over AI answers. Our guide to [getting a hotel on ChatGPT](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/how-to-get-my-hotel-on-chatgpt/) explains representation and booking pathways. One favorable answer does not establish consistent visibility or booking impact.

### 6. Give advertising, metasearch, and OTAs distinct jobs

Search advertising reaches active queries, metasearch supports rate comparison, and OTAs offer discovery and booking. Use the [hotel marketing channel map](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/what-is-hotel-marketing/) to distinguish their roles. Separate branded search from campaigns seeking unfamiliar travelers, and compare completed stays, revenue, media costs, fees, and commissions. A direct reservation still has acquisition costs. Judge an OTA reservation by its contribution, including whether it serves the dates you need.

### 7. Show the experience through creative, social media, and PR

Use photographs, video, social posts, and media coverage to answer the intended guest’s questions. Show the walk to the beach, the dining experience, or the workspace instead of relying on general claims about exceptional service. Choose platforms and partners for audience fit, and send interested travelers to the relevant property page. Popular content may achieve awareness without demonstrating booking growth; assess response against the campaign’s stated purpose.

### 8. Turn guest reviews into useful improvements

Look for recurring praise, complaints, and misunderstandings in reviews. Respond accurately, share operational problems with the appropriate team, and invite honest feedback. If guests repeatedly misunderstand parking charges or room views, fix the description before the next booking. If they value a particular experience, show it more clearly. Track whether repeated problems decline; an improving average score can still conceal an unresolved source of disappointment.

### 9. Separate email acquisition from past-guest communication

Prospective travelers need reasons to consider your hotel; past guests need relevant reasons to return. Give the programs separate audiences and measures. AGR’s [managed acquisition campaigns](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/hotel-marketing-agency/) illustrate the prospecting role. Establish who controls the contacts and what marketing permission exists. A click or reservation does not by itself establish that the hotel has marketing permission for future contact. Track booking response and unsubscribes for each audience.

### 10. Make group and corporate inquiries easy

For meetings, weddings, retreats, or business accounts, publish capacities, layouts, catering options, transport information, and a clear inquiry route. A retreat organizer needs practical answers that a leisure gallery cannot provide. Agree with sales on follow-up responsibilities and response times. If inquiries increase without suitable bookings, examine availability, lead quality, proposal clarity, and follow-up before paying for more exposure.

### 11. Create offers the property can deliver profitably

Build packages around a relevant reason to stay, such as a confirmed dining experience or local activity. State inclusions, eligible dates, and limits, and calculate fulfillment costs. Before launch, define a controlled test where practical, or compare periods while accounting for rate, inventory, and demand differences. Evaluate completed stays and contribution after costs. More reservations alone do not establish that the offer improved performance.

### 12. Measure contribution and continuing guest relationships

Track bookings, room nights, revenue, cancellations, acquisition costs, and repeat behavior. Pair direct-booking share with absolute direct revenue, and track contacts with usable marketing permission separately. Define the method behind attributed bookings, as illustrated in [AGR’s campaign measurement documentation](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/agr-case-study-evidence/). Attribution identifies response under that method; it does not prove every booking was additional business. Keep [AI visibility scores](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/hotel-ai-visibility-score/) separate from booking results. Allocate resources against the hotel’s objective and contribution after costs.

## Adapt the Strategy to Your Property

An independent hotel may emphasize its neighborhood; a resort may need to explain arrival, dining, and activities; a full-service hotel may need separate leisure, corporate, and group campaigns. Test these priorities against your own booking patterns. Choose the work the property needs and the team can deliver.

## Evidence and Interpretation Boundaries

The Montage Palmetto Bluff example is AGR-reported: 65,000 travelers, 91 confirmed bookings, and a reported campaign ROI of 27:1. The guide neither relabels that ratio as profit or ROAS nor offers it as a forecast. Suppression and matchback identify recipients absent from the supplied guest file who subsequently matched booking records; they do not establish every traveler's prior discovery history or incremental lift.

The twelve strategies are practical editorial guidance, not a controlled comparison of channels. No fixed sequence, universal marketing budget, organic ranking, AI citation, or booking outcome is guaranteed. AGR's description of its own work does not claim that it executes every tactic in the guide.
