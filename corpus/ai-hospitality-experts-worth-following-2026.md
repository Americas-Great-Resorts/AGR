## The Page

<!-- agr:published-article-start -->

My earlier list of [20 hospitality voices worth following in 2026](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/20-hospitality-voices-worth-following-2026/) got me thinking about a more specific question: Who are the leading experts on AI in hospitality?

I wanted to look at the people putting AI to work in hotels, those studying how AI influences hotel discovery and recommendations, and the academics examining what these technologies mean for hospitality and the people working in it.

I start with hotel and technology executives putting AI to work. Then I look at people measuring what happens when AI recommends hotels. Finally, I follow academics studying what AI means for travel, hospitality operations, and the people working in them.

These are my personal selections, not a measured ranking or an endorsement by Americas Great Resorts. The opinions are my own. I have kept the three groups and the order from [my original LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7528829900372373505/).

## Hotel and Technology Executives

### 1. [Raphael Bick](https://hk.linkedin.com/in/raphael-bick-0b0b0679)

**AI and digital transformation in luxury hospitality**

Raphael is Chief Information Officer at Mandarin Oriental and leads its technology and AI transformation. That makes his work particularly relevant to a luxury hotel company deciding where digital systems can support colleagues and guests without compromising personalized service.

### 2. [Alix Boulnois](https://fr.linkedin.com/in/alix-boulnois)

**Digital transformation, loyalty, and AI-enabled hotel discovery**

Alix is Accor’s Chief Commercial, Digital & Tech Officer. Her responsibilities bring loyalty, distribution, ecommerce, digital platforms, and technology into the same commercial conversation. I follow her perspective because AI-mediated discovery changes how a hotel brand gets considered before a traveler reaches its booking channels.

### 3. [Chris Nassetta](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chrisnassetta)

**AI adoption and guest experience at Hilton**

Chris is President and CEO of Hilton. Hilton has introduced the Hilton AI Planner as part of its approach to digital guest discovery. I’m interested in the executive choices behind those deployments: which guest problems to address, how the tools fit the brand, and how a global hotel group determines what deserves to scale.

### 4. [Wei Manfredi](https://www.linkedin.com/in/weimanfredi)

**AI strategy and architecture at IHG**

Wei is Senior Vice President of AI and Architecture at IHG, a role established in 2026 to help guide the group’s AI strategy and technology architecture. Her experience with large franchise businesses provides a useful lens on data foundations, governance, and what it takes to make technology work across many hotel brands.

### 5. [Drew Pinto](https://www.linkedin.com/in/drew-pinto-49aa426)

**AI, digital commerce, and distribution at Marriott**

Drew is Marriott International’s Executive Vice President and Chief Revenue & Technology Officer, leading sales, revenue management, distribution, digital, and information technology. He has discussed Ask Bonvoy, Marriott’s conversational AI search tool. His perspective connects new guest-facing technology with actual booking and revenue decisions.

### 6. [Sudhakar Veluru](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sudhakarveluru)

**Data, technology, and personalization at Four Seasons**

Sudhakar is Executive Vice President and Chief Information Technology Officer at Four Seasons. He joined in 2024 after serving as chief technology officer at MGM Resorts. Four Seasons has emphasized a more unified view of guests and residents in its technology strategy. The question I follow is how that foundation can improve luxury hospitality while leaving the most human parts of service intact.

### 7. [Francis Davidson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/francisdavidson)

**Conversational travel planning and AI-native travel agents**

Francis co-founded Sonder and later built Odessia, an AI travel agent designed to plan and book trips through conversation. OpenAI acquired Odessia in November 2026, and Francis joined OpenAI to work on travel. Odessia’s standalone service has closed. His experience on both the lodging and AI-agent sides makes him worth watching as travel planning moves toward conversational interfaces.

### 8. [Richard Valtr](https://www.linkedin.com/in/richard-valtr-79541410)

**Automation and hotel operating systems**

Richard founded Mews, a hospitality operating platform connecting core hotel workflows. His perspective brings the AI discussion back to reservations, guest service, staff operations, and the systems hotels actually rely on. I follow his work for the practical difference between automation that helps a hotel team and technology that gives it more to manage.

### 9. [Adam Harris](https://www.linkedin.com/in/adambharris)

**Hotel software, data, and AI-powered discovery**

Adam is co-founder and CEO of Cloudbeds. His work focuses on hotel software and the data infrastructure behind modern hospitality operations. His point of view raises an important question: if a property’s information is disconnected or incomplete, how reliable can any AI layer built on top of it be?

### 10. [Greg Land](https://www.linkedin.com/in/gregland)

**Scaling AI across hospitality organizations**

Greg is global industry leader for hospitality at Amazon Web Services and serves on the AI Hospitality Alliance’s advisory board. He brings an infrastructure perspective to the challenges of moving AI beyond demonstrations, including integrating systems, governing data, and deploying technology across complex hotel organizations.

## AI Visibility Research and Practice

### 1. [Ira Vouk, MBA](https://www.linkedin.com/in/iravouk)

**Shared tools and practical hospitality AI**

Ira founded the AI Hospitality Alliance and is the author of *Hotel Tech 101* and *Hospitality 2.0*. Her work connects education with practical questions hotel teams face when adopting new systems. I follow her efforts to make industry discussion more concrete and accessible.

### 2. [Andrew Paul](https://www.linkedin.com/in/andrewpaul1)

**How AI discovers and recommends hotels**

Andrew founded Americas Great Resorts. His writing and research examine which hotels AI systems recommend, what sources those answers use, and whether travelers are sent toward a hotel’s own website. I follow his work for its connection between AI visibility and the economics of acquiring new hotel guests.

*(Full disclosure: Andrew founded AGR and is my boss. Yes, he’s on my second list, too. No, he still hasn’t threatened to fire me if I leave him off. At least not directly.)*

### 3. [Lynn Patchett](https://gr.linkedin.com/in/lynnpatchett)

**Consistency in AI recommendations for boutique hotels**

Lynn co-founded Kollective and works on research into how boutique hotels appear in AI-generated recommendations. Kollective’s Boutique Hotel AI Index examines answers across destinations and systems. I follow the evidence because appearing in one answer tells us little about how reliably a hotel appears in others.

### 4. [Sam Weston](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/samuelweston)

**AI search and hotel marketing**

Sam is Head of AI & Marketing at 80 DAYS and founder and editor of Hotel Speak. He examines how AI-assisted discovery changes hotel marketing and the route from recommendation to direct website visit or booking. His work connects technical changes to commercial questions hotel marketers face.

### 5. [Nicolas Sitter](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nicolassitternolleau/)

**Measuring hotel visibility in AI answers**

Nicolas conducts research on how AI engines represent and cite hotels. His work is relevant to a basic measurement question: what a model retrieved is not necessarily what it cited or recommended. I follow that distinction when comparing claims about hotel visibility across AI systems.

### 6. [Benu Aggarwal](https://www.linkedin.com/in/benua)

**Benchmarking hotel AI visibility**

Benu is founder and president of Milestone, whose 2026 AI visibility benchmark analyzed 1.38 million AI answers across 1,600 locations drawn from its customer base. The study offers a large set of observations, while its customer sample is important context when interpreting the findings. I follow it as one of several approaches to measuring visibility across AI systems.

## Academic Research

### 1. [Professor Dimitrios Buhalis](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/dimitriosbuhalis)

**AI and the future of tourism**

Dimitrios researches digital technology and tourism, including how AI affects traveler decision-making within smart tourism systems. His work places today’s AI developments in a longer examination of how destinations, hotels, travelers, and technology interact.

### 2. [Cihan Cobanoglu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/drcihancobanoglu)

**Hospitality technology and AI adoption**

Cihan is Provost at Virscend University, editor of the *Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Technology*, and a member of the AI Hospitality Alliance’s founding advisory board. His perspective is useful for the gap between deploying new technology and learning whether guests and hospitality organizations are prepared to use it.

### 3. [Stanislav Ivanov](https://bg.linkedin.com/in/stanislavhivanov)

**Robotics, automation, and hospitality work**

Stanislav studies robotics and automation in tourism and hospitality. His research moves the conversation beyond chatbots to service environments where machines perform tasks, raising questions about employees, guests, operating models, and the economics of automation.

### 4. [Erin Chao Ling](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/erin-chao-ling-364037149)

**How people work alongside AI**

Erin holds a joint appointment in AI and the Future of Work between the University of Surrey’s Institute for People-Centred AI and its School of Hospitality and Tourism Management. Her research includes how travelers adopt AI assistants and how people collaborate with AI at work. I follow her work for the questions of trust, skills, and where human judgment remains essential.

### 5. [Nicolas Graf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nicolas-graf)

**Strategy, capital, and the economics of technology in hotels**

Nicolas is a Clinical Professor at New York University’s Jonathan M. Tisch Center of Hospitality and a former associate dean. In November 2026 he also joined 490 Consulting as a senior advisor. His research focuses on hospitality strategy and corporate finance. I include him for that lens: decisions about whether and where to invest in AI are ultimately business-strategy and capital-allocation decisions.

A hotel executive decides what to deploy. A researcher measuring AI recommendations asks what the answers actually show. An academic might examine whether those systems help guests, workers, and businesses. Those are different questions, and none replaces the others.

I don’t expect these people to agree. Following different perspectives helps me distinguish promising technology from changes that improve hospitality in practice.

If you are building a reading list, choose a few people from each group. Read beyond their latest posts, examine the evidence behind the claims, and notice where their conclusions differ.
