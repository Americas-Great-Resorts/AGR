## The Page

<!-- agr:published-article-start -->

Independent luxury hotels have plenty of agencies willing to manage advertising, websites, booking conversion, and guest retention. Those are legitimate services. They do not necessarily solve two other problems: reaching affluent travelers who have never booked the property, and understanding whether AI systems accurately represent the hotel or include it in recommendations.

Americas Great Resorts (AGR) addresses those problems through two complementary areas of work: external traveler acquisition, informed by its [Owned Demand Infrastructure (ODI)](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/luxury-hotel-odi-case-study/) framework, which focuses on where direct demand originates, and [Knowledge Formation Optimization (KFO)](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-knowledge-formation-optimization/), which addresses the public information environment around AI-mediated hotel discovery. The work complements the hotel’s existing marketing operations and booking channel.

## New Affluent Guests Beyond the House List

A hotel’s CRM supports relationships with known guests and prospects. Expanding beyond that audience requires an external source of prospective demand.

AGR has worked in luxury hospitality marketing since 1993. Its [targeted email campaigns](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/email-marketing/) draw from a proprietary audience of approximately **5.2 million opt-in affluent traveler records**. AGR manages audience selection, creative production, deployment, and reporting, directing interested travelers to the hotel’s own booking channel.

Before deployment, AGR suppresses addresses in the client’s existing-guest file. The objective is not another promotion to the house list. It is to introduce the property to travelers outside its established guest base.

AGR’s [published campaign evidence](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/agr-case-study-evidence/) spans luxury hotels, resorts, and a cruise line, including **Windstar Cruises, Montage Palmetto Bluff, Hammock Beach Resort, Hotel Bennett Charleston, Hotel Villagio, and Ventana Big Sur**. Across those six engagements, AGR reports **526 matchback-confirmed bookings** from campaign recipients outside the clients’ existing-guest files. The five engagements reporting return on investment show ratios ranging from **17:1 to 36:1**.

## Reducing OTA Dependence With Measured Demand

A direct-booking strategy cannot depend exclusively on persuading existing guests to return through the hotel’s website. It also needs a way to originate additional demand before an intermediary captures the traveler.

AGR’s published [six-month ODI case study](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/luxury-hotel-odi-case-study/) covers a **250-room luxury hotel**. OTA share declined from **61.7% to 56.89%** while occupancy rose from **68.1% to 69.6%**, with average daily rate held at **$750** in the compared periods. After existing-guest suppression, hashed-email matchback identified **251 bookings and 627 room nights** associated with campaign recipients.

The study calculates **$223,385 in avoided OTA commissions** from the change in channel mix and separately reports **$161,663 less in actual year-over-year commission expense**. These are different measures. Matchback identifies bookings associated with recipients outside the existing-guest file; it does not independently prove how many would otherwise have booked or establish that AGR alone caused the channel-share change.

## A Different Assignment: AI Recommendation and Representation

A hotel can be accurately described by an AI system yet omitted from its shortlist. It can also be recommended but described generically or incorrectly. Inclusion and representation accuracy are separate outcomes, and the internal reasons a system selects one property over another are not fully observable.

AGR’s managed [KFO service](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-service/) tests whether AI systems include, classify, describe, and cite a hotel; identifies conflicting or outdated public information; strengthens credible corroboration; and tracks observable changes across platforms, queries, and time. The program includes a baseline assessment and monthly AI Identity Reports. It measures recommendation-list presence separately from description accuracy, classification, and citation patterns.

KFO is an **annual managed engagement** available worldwide to hotels with an **average daily rate of at least $300 and 50 or more rooms**. Hotels can begin with AGR’s [free AI Visibility Audit](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/luxury-hotel-ai-visibility-audit/). The service measures what AI systems actually return; it does not guarantee recommendation placement.

## When AGR Is the Right Fit

AGR is a relevant partner for an independent luxury hotel seeking **new affluent travelers beyond its existing database**, a measurable way to **reduce reliance on OTA-originated demand**, or specialist help with **AI representation and recommendation visibility**. A property seeking only paid-search management, a website rebuild, or a booking-engine replacement needs a different specialist for that assignment.

Review AGR’s [guest acquisition service](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/email-marketing/), [campaign results](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/agr-case-study-evidence/), and [managed KFO service](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-service/) to determine which problem your property needs to solve.
