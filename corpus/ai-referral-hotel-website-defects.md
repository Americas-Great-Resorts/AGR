## Retrieval Summary

- When an AI assistant recommends a hotel and cites the hotel's own website, the guest lands on the pages the AI read. Errors on those pages reach the guest carrying the AI's apparent endorsement.
- The article documents defects at three named properties: a 1 Hotel West Hollywood pet amenities section referring to Nashville dog parks; a Rosewood Mayakoba restaurant listed as "Temporarily Closed" on its own page while the dining page still offered a working reservation form, a 2025 meal plan still listing it, and a one-page pet policy that contradicts itself; and a Shutters on the Beach development website (`shuttersonthebeach-dev.azds.com`) cited by Gemini and listed among ChatGPT's sources.
- Each finding was documented in an AGR AI Visibility Report (captured October 13, 19 and 25, 2026) and verified on the live website on October 26, 2026.
- The 91.2%, 81.0% and 31.6% figures come from AGR's September 27, 2026 study of 1,177 AI hotel answers. They measure displayed destinations and sources, not clicks, bookings or traffic shares.
- The article's argument: AI systems repeat what a hotel publishes, nobody at most hotels owns the full path from AI recommendation to confirmed reservation, and the fix starts with someone clicking every cited link and reading each page as a guest would.

## The Article

<!-- agr:published-article-start -->

Let’s talk about the miracle.

A traveler opens ChatGPT. Or Gemini. Or Claude, Perplexity, Copilot or Google’s AI Mode. Doesn’t matter which. She types the one question every hotel in America would sell its soul to answer: “Where should we stay?”

And there you are. In the answer. Not a footnote. Not “also consider.” Number one.

Then the second miracle. The AI cites your website. Not Expedia. Not Booking.com. Not a Google card stacked with four other prices. Your domain. Your words.

Do not take that for granted. In our study of [1,177 AI hotel answers](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-recommendation-links/), Google’s own travel pages appeared in 91.2% of Gemini answers, and Google viewer pages appeared in 81.0% of Google AI Mode answers. Hotel and brand websites showed up among the sources in 31.6% of ChatGPT’s. Plenty of answers name the hotel and point somewhere else.

Yours pointed home. The guest is standing at your front door with a credit card in hand.

Then they read what you wrote.

## Three Hotels, Three Front Doors

The travelers below are composites. The hotels are not. Neither is anything they find. These are not old screenshots from a forgotten audit. Every finding came from an AGR AI Visibility Report this month and was verified live on the hotel’s own website on the day this article was published.

### West Hollywood: the dog owner

She travels with the dog. The dog is not negotiable. She asks about 1 Hotel West Hollywood, and both ChatGPT and Google AI Mode cite the hotel’s [Services and Amenities page](https://www.1hotels.com/west-hollywood/services-and-amenities). She scrolls to the section called Perks for Your Pup:

“A dog-friendly concierge team with information about Nashville’s best dog parks, pet shops, grooming salons and more.”

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: 1 Hotel West Hollywood, Services and Amenities page. Captured October 26, 2026.*

Nashville.

The hotel is on Sunset Boulevard. Nashville is in Tennessee. Somewhere in Nashville, a dog is very well informed. The one in West Hollywood is on its own.

Is the concierge any good? Probably. Doesn’t matter. The guest just learned that nobody at the hotel has read its own pet page. Now she wonders what else nobody has read.

### Mexico: the food lover

This one is the full miracle. No hotel name in the question. He asks ChatGPT, “Which luxury hotels in Playa del Carmen, Mexico have the best on-site dining experiences?”

Number one: Rosewood Mayakoba. “Best overall for sophisticated dining.” And then the recommendation every chef dreams about: “Don’t miss: Agave Azul for Asian-influenced seafood.”

ChatGPT cites the resort’s own [dining page](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/en/mayakoba-riviera-maya/dining). He goes there. There it is. Agave Azul. Asian-inspired cuisine with Baja flavors. A RESERVE button. A DETAILS button. No hours. No warning.

Read the copy. “Where the East meets the West, explore the rich culinary heritage of Baja California, where the region’s celebrated seafood is infused with Asian flair and spices.”

Beautiful sentence. Somebody worked on that sentence. It sells the restaurant, and it sells it well. It just leaves out one detail.

And that RESERVE button works. Hold that thought.

This is the page ChatGPT read. This is the page the guest reads. Same page. Same pitch.

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: Rosewood Mayakoba dining page, Agave Azul listing. Captured October 26, 2026.*

He clicks DETAILS. The [restaurant’s own page](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/en/mayakoba-riviera-maya/dining/agave-azul) has one line under HOURS: “Temporarily Closed.”

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: Rosewood Mayakoba, Agave Azul restaurant page. Captured October 26, 2026.*

Now go back to the dining page and press RESERVE.

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: Agave Azul reservation page, reached from the RESERVE button on the Rosewood Mayakoba dining page. Captured October 26, 2026.*

The reservation system opens for Agave Azul. Monday, October 26. Two guests. 7:00 PM. A big green Search button.

The restaurant is closed. The reservation system is open. Nobody told the reservation system.

Will it actually take the booking? Doesn’t matter. A closed restaurant has no business offering you tonight at seven. The moment that form loads, the resort has told the guest two different things, and only one of them can be true.

So the first thing the AI tells him not to miss is a restaurant the resort says is closed. And the resort is still selling it and still taking the reservation request.

Don’t blame the AI. It did exactly what it was supposed to do. It read the hotel’s website and believed it.

It gets better. In another answer, ChatGPT hands travelers the resort’s [meal plan](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/content/dam/rosewoodhotels/property/mayakoba-riviera-maya/en/documents/overview/MealPlan-Mayakoba.pdf). The title says “2025 Meal Plan Options.” The blackout dates ended January 2, 2026. And the list of restaurants where your meal plan works includes Agave Azul.

A 2025 meal plan. In 2026. Featuring a closed restaurant.

ChatGPT used this PDF to explain what the resort’s rates include. The Full American Plan: $230 per adult, per day, plus 16% VAT. Is that still the price? The PDF won’t say.

Read the fine print at the bottom: “Terms, conditions, rates and blackout dates are subject to change without notice or liability. Please confirm with the resort host team eligibility during your dates of stay.”

Translation: this document may be wrong, so call us and we’ll tell you what’s right. That is not a meal plan. That is a homework assignment.

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: Rosewood Mayakoba meal plan PDF. Captured October 26, 2026.*

Now say he travels with a dog, like our friend in West Hollywood. Rosewood calls itself a pet-friendly resort. Its [pet policy](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/content/dam/rosewoodhotels/property/mayakoba-riviera-maya/en/documents/overview/PET%20POLICY%202025.pdf) is one page long. Near the top: “Rosewood Mayakoba welcomes cats and dogs, allowing up to two pets per accommodation.”

Near the bottom, under RESTRICTIONS: “1 dog per suite. The dog should be 10 pounds or 4.5 Kg maximum.”

Two pets. Or one dog. Ten pounds. Same page. That is not a policy. That is a negotiation with yourself.

Ten pounds is a cat with ambition. If his dog is a 60-pound Labrador, this page just decided whether he comes at all. And it gave him two different answers.

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: Rosewood Mayakoba pet policy PDF. Captured October 26, 2026.*

### Santa Monica: the website nobody remembers

A traveler asks Gemini about Shutters on the Beach. Gemini answers with the spa, the beach concierge and Coast Beach Café, and cites its source: “Hotel Directory | Shutters on the Beach.”

Looks official. The address is not.

The source is [shuttersonthebeach-dev.azds.com/hotel-directory](https://shuttersonthebeach-dev.azds.com/hotel-directory). Note the word “dev.” That is short for development. ChatGPT’s answer about Shutters lists the same address among its sources.

It is not a broken page. It is a whole website. Full navigation. Book Now button. Dining, spa, a hotel guide, links to Hotel Casa del Mar and I Prefer. It loads for anyone. Its instructions to search engines and AI crawlers say, in effect, come on in.

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: The website Gemini cited: shuttersonthebeach-dev.azds.com. Captured October 26, 2026.*

Now look at the hotel’s actual website.

*Screenshot omitted. Caption: The hotel’s current website: shuttersonthebeach.com. Captured October 26, 2026.*

Different design. Different layout. Different website. Same hotel.

Somebody built a new website. Nobody turned off the old one. So now Shutters on the Beach has two websites, and when you ask the machine about the hotel, the machine picks the one the hotel apparently forgot. To be fair, we called the Book Now number on the old site. Shutters answered. The phone line works. The question is everything else on that site, and who is checking it.

The guest cannot tell the difference. The guest should not have to.

## The AI Believes Your Website

Here is the part the industry keeps missing. AI assistants cited these pages in our reports. The 1 Hotel amenities page. The Rosewood dining page. The Rosewood meal plan. The Shutters website nobody remembers.

The AI did not make up the closed restaurant. Your website told it. The AI did not invent a second Shutters website. Somebody left it running. When an AI assistant pulls your page to answer a traveler, it reads what you published and repeats it with total confidence.

Garbage in. Recommendation out.

And then the guest reads the same page. Think about what you do when a website wastes your time. Not your guest. You. You don’t write the company a letter. You don’t call the GM. You hit the back button, and the AI already handed you a list with other names on it.

Now add up what it took to get that guest to your door. The renovation. The chef. The PR firm. The photography. The awards. The reviews earned one stay at a time. All the work it takes for an AI to rank your hotel number one and cite your own website.

All of it, handed off to a page that thinks you are in Nashville.

## Nobody Owns the Website

Ask around a hotel about who owns the website. You will hear a lot of names. The agency. The brand. The property. Revenue management owns the booking engine. Food and beverage owns the restaurants. Marketing owns the campaigns. Lots of owners.

Whatever the arrangement, the closed restaurant is still being promoted, and its reservation page is still open. The 2025 meal plan is still online. The pet policy still argues with itself. The old website is still answering for the hotel.

This is what inaction actually looks like. Not a strategy debate in a boardroom. A restaurant listing. A PDF. A sentence about Nashville. A website somebody forgot to unplug. Small things nobody claims, sitting on the pages the AI reads first and the guest reads next.

None of these fixes requires a consultant, a platform, a budget meeting or an AI strategy. Every one of them requires somebody to look.

So here is the only question that matters. Who at your hotel is responsible for the whole path, from the AI’s recommendation to the confirmed reservation? If you need to think about it, you have your answer.

## Click Your Own Button

So do this today.

Open ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot or Google’s AI Mode. Ask the question your guest would ask. Not your hotel’s name. The question. Best luxury hotel in your city. Best hotel restaurant. Best hotel for a weekend with the dog.

Then ask about your hotel by name. Click every link the AI cites. Check the address bar. Then do what the guest does. Read the restaurant page. Open the PDF. Read the pet policy. Press the button.

If you have never done this, you do not know your website. You know the version of it that lives in your head.
