AI visibility for hotels is whether ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Mode, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot or any other AI assistant names your hotel when a traveler asks where to stay, describes it correctly and sends the traveler somewhere useful. The answers are concentrated. Across 824 hotel recommendation slots captured from ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Gemini in six US luxury markets, 152 hotels were named at least once and 23 of them received half of all 824 slots.

That is a short list doing most of the talking. The other 129 named hotels split the remaining half. Hotels never named got nothing. And the standard fix most hotels are being sold, schema markup and an llms.txt file, showed no detectable association with how often a hotel was recommended in AGR’s data.

This page covers what AGR has measured across more than 1,700 AI hotel answers in three separate studies: what AI visibility is, how to check your own hotel, who gets recommended, why hotels go missing and what moves the result.

## What AI Visibility for Hotels Actually Measures

Being “visible” in AI is three separate things. A hotel can win one and lose the other two.

1. **Recognition.** Ask about the hotel by name. Does the AI describe it accurately?
2. **Recommendation.** Ask the question a traveler asks before they know the name: best honeymoon resort in Maui, best boutique hotel in Chicago. Is the hotel in the answer?
3. **Routing.** When the hotel is named, where does the link go? The hotel’s own website, a publisher’s review, an online travel agency, or the AI platform’s own travel interface?

A hotel can pass the first test and fail the second. In a June 2026 audit, AGR tested a recently opened adults-only luxury resort on six AI platforms. Asked to describe the resort by name, every platform got it right. Asked for the best adults-only resort in its destination, every platform left it out and named the same few long-established competitors. On a major review site’s best-value ranking for that exact category, the resort ranked first, above those competitors. The ranking existed. The platforms named the others anyway.

Recognition proves the AI knows you exist. Recommendation decides whether a traveler ever hears your name.

## How to Check Your Hotel’s AI Visibility in Ten Minutes

You can run a first check yourself. Use a logged-out or private browser window so past chats don’t shape the answer.

1. **Test recognition.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode to describe your hotel by name. Note anything wrong: amenities, room count, restaurants, status.
2. **Test recommendation.** Ask three questions a first-time guest would ask: the best luxury hotel in your destination, the best hotel for your strongest occasion (honeymoon, family, wellness), and the best hotel in your neighborhood. Note whether you appear and who appears instead.
3. **Ask each question twice.** Answers vary even on the same day. One answer is a sample, not a verdict.
4. **Check the links.** When you are named, where does the link go: your site, a publisher, an online travel agency or a Google travel page?
5. **Click your own link.** If the AI links your website, read that page as a guest would. Is it current?

If you are recognized but not recommended, look at the sources the AI shows next to the hotels it named instead. If you are recommended but described wrong, compare the wrong description with the sources the AI displays. Either way, the answer is rarely on your homepage alone.

## Which Hotels AI Recommends Most Often

**A few hotels take most of the answers.** In AGR’s [Luxury Hotel AI Visibility Index](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-visibility-index/), captured July 29, 2026 across 180 answers, five properties held half of all recommendations in the average market. In Chicago, Maui and Napa Valley, four did.

**The same hotels keep showing up.** In the [ten-destination study](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-recommendations-research/) on September 27, 2026, AGR asked 100 travel questions three times each on ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini and Copilot, producing 1,177 assessed answers. The Peninsula Chicago appeared in 96 of 118 Chicago answers. Halekulani appeared in 91 of 118 Honolulu answers and led on all four engines.

**The leaders mostly held five weeks later.** On November 2, AGR asked the same 100 questions again. In the [five-week follow-up](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-recommendations-five-weeks-later/), 7 of 10 destinations kept the same leading hotel.

**The engine matters.** In 9 of 10 destinations, the four engines did not share the same leading hotel. Winning in ChatGPT does not mean winning in Gemini.

**The question matters.** In Orlando, the most-featured hotels for family vacations, boutique stays and Walt Disney World trips were different hotels. A hotel can lead for one traveler need and be absent for the next.

Leading hotels often kept their visibility. Complete recommendation lists changed constantly, even between answers collected the same day.

## Why Hotels Go Missing from AI Answers

### Third-party publishers dominate the sources AI shows

When AI systems show their sources for a hotel recommendation, most of those sources are not hotel websites. In the ten-destination study, publishers and editorial guides appeared in 74.6% of ChatGPT answers. Hotel or brand websites appeared in 31.6%. An answer could include both. In the July Index, the same few list pages recurred market after market: Michelin Guide lists in ChatGPT’s Los Angeles answers, Tripadvisor lists in its Chicago answers, one lifestyle-publisher listicle across Gemini’s New York, Los Angeles and Miami answers.

A displayed source does not prove what caused a recommendation. It does show where the answer points. When the sources shown for your category are lists built around other hotels, a technically perfect website does not guarantee a place in the answer.

### Credentials carry weight a website cannot fake

AGR’s [recommendation study](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/luxury-hotel-ai-recommendation-study/) examined 148 luxury hotels already named at least once in the Index, accounting for 816 of its 824 slots. A model containing Forbes Travel Guide rating, Michelin Key count and market accounted for 54.7% of the variation in log recommendation slot count. Adding editorial coverage raised that to 59.4%. Tripadvisor review volume, Wikipedia presence and AAA Five Diamond status added no detectable independent signal once those credentials were in the model.

Separately, in three ChatGPT sessions on September 8, Forbes and Michelin pages made up 80 to 100% of the visible retrieval record.

Credentials are strongly associated with recommendation frequency. They are neither required nor enough. Fairmont Kea Lani in Maui had no Forbes rating and no Michelin Key and received 14 slots. Crosby Street Hotel in New York held three Michelin Keys, a Forbes Four-Star rating and 48 measured editorial pages and received none.

### Wrong facts travel as easily as right ones

AI answers can carry outdated, wrong or unsupported hotel facts.

- **A demolished hotel kept getting recommended.** On July 29, 2026, ChatGPT and Google AI Mode recommended Mandarin Oriental, Miami five times. It closed on May 31, 2025. The building was demolished on April 12, 2026, 108 days before the capture.
- **A competitor got credit for your award.** In one audit, two AI platforms told travelers a competitor held the only Forbes Five-Star restaurant in the state. The audited hotel’s restaurant held the same rating, awarded the same cycle. The sources shown with those answers were the competitor’s own dining pages and culinary roundups.
- **The citation did not support the claim.** In AGR’s [review of 80 Perplexity hotel answers](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/perplexity-hotel-citations-study/), 272 claim-and-citation pairs attached a fact to a page that did not establish it.

Guest scores did not settle it either. One audited property held a 9.8 guest score, the top in its state on a major platform. On the broad luxury question, the AI showed editorial roundups that left it out, and the property was absent on every platform.

## Does Schema or llms.txt Get Your Hotel Recommended?

AGR found no statistically detectable association between recommendation frequency and either lodging-type schema markup (p = 0.23) or an llms.txt file (p = 0.40) among 148 luxury hotels already recommended by AI.

That matters because adding structured data, publishing llms.txt and opening your site to AI crawlers is the advice on almost every other page about AI visibility for hotels. It is reasonable hygiene. AGR tested whether it predicts recommendations. AGR crawled the websites of those 148 hotels on September 6, 2026, using recommendations captured in the July 29 Index:

| Technical signal | Hotels with it | Association with recommendation frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Lodging-type schema markup | 109 of 148 (73.6%) | None detected (+0.7 slots, p = 0.23) |
| llms.txt file | 39 of 148 (26.4%) | None detected (-0.5 slots, p = 0.40) |
| Blocked any AI crawler in robots.txt | 2 of 148 (1.4%) | Too few to test |

Hotels with llms.txt were recommended slightly less often than hotels without one. Neither difference was statistically detectable. The full analysis is in [Do AI-Recommended Luxury Hotels Use Schema and llms.txt?](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-recommended-luxury-hotel-schema-llms-txt/)

The study measured hotels already in the recommendation set. It did not test whether schema helps a hotel get into the set in the first place, and it does not make these signals useless for crawling or factual accuracy. Keep them. Fix them if they are broken. Just don’t expect them to do the work of the third-party record.

## Where AI Hotel Recommendations Actually Link

Getting named is half the win. The other half is where the traveler goes next. In the ten-destination study:

- **Gemini** linked to Google Travel in 91.2% of answers. The card carried the hotel’s name. The link stayed on Google.
- **Google AI Mode** used Google viewer links in 81.0% of answers. Inline links tied to hotel or brand websites appeared in at least 10.7%.
- **ChatGPT** cited publishers far more often than hotel websites, 74.6% against 31.6%.

These figures describe where the links pointed, not clicks or bookings. The commercial consequence is laid out in [You Won the AI Recommendation. Expedia Won the Click.](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-link-trap/)

And when the AI does send a guest to your own website, the guest lands on the pages the AI linked. If those pages list a closed restaurant or a pet policy that contradicts itself, the AI just walked a traveler into it. AGR documented exactly that at named properties in [AI Parted the Red Sea for Your Hotel. Then Your Website Drowned the Guest.](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-referral-hotel-website-defects/)

## How to Improve AI Visibility for Hotels

The research points to the public record around the hotel, not the hotel website alone. In practice:

1. **Find and fix the sources.** Identify the sources AI shows for the questions where competitors win and how they describe those competitors. Correct what is wrong in the records the hotel controls and request corrections in the ones it doesn’t.
2. **Make real distinctions clear and consistent** across the hotel’s website, collection listings, destination resources and editorial coverage. Credentials must be earned under the publishers’ rules. Clear facts make earned credentials count.
3. **Keep the hotel’s own pages current,** because when AI does link the website, those pages carry the AI’s endorsement to the guest.
4. **Re-ask the same questions** across the same engines and track inclusion, accuracy and links over time. Lists vary from one answer to the next, so one check proves nothing either way.

SEO, structured data, PR and listing management all contribute. None of them alone covers the third-party record. AGR calls this discipline [Knowledge Formation Optimization (KFO)](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-knowledge-formation-optimization/): correcting and strengthening the public record AI systems draw from, then measuring whether the answers change. The [KFO Service](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-service/) page explains how AGR runs that work for hotels and resorts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is AI visibility for hotels?

AI visibility for hotels is whether AI assistants name a hotel when travelers ask for recommendations, describe it accurately and link to a useful destination. It has three parts: recognition by name, inclusion in recommendations and where the links lead.

### Why isn’t my hotel showing up in ChatGPT?

Test it by name first. If ChatGPT describes your hotel correctly but leaves it out of recommendations, examine the third-party sources it shows for the hotels it named instead. In AGR’s research, publishers and editorial guides appeared in 74.6% of ChatGPT hotel answers and hotel websites in 31.6%.

### Does schema markup or llms.txt get a hotel recommended by AI?

Not detectably, in AGR’s data. Among 148 luxury hotels already recommended by AI, neither lodging schema nor llms.txt was associated with how often the hotel was recommended. The study did not test whether either affects initial inclusion, crawling or factual accuracy. Neither substitutes for the third-party record.

### Do AI tools recommend the same hotels every time?

The leaders mostly hold: 7 of 10 destinations kept the same leading hotel over five weeks. The rest of the list changes constantly. Only 16 of 379 question-and-engine groups returned the same hotel set three times on the same day.

### How do I check my hotel’s AI visibility?

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode to describe your hotel, then ask the questions a first-time guest asks, twice each, and note who appears and where the links go. AGR’s [real-time AI Visibility Report](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-visibility-test/) runs that check across all three engines.

### Is AI visibility the same as hotel SEO?

No. A hotel can rank on page one of Google and be absent from AI recommendations. SEO helps a hotel’s own pages get found and cited. AI recommendations lean heavily on third-party lists, guides and credentials that a hotel’s SEO does not control.

### Can anyone guarantee a hotel will be recommended by AI?

No. AI platforms and independent publishers make their own decisions. What can be done is to correct and strengthen the public record behind the answers and measure whether inclusion, accuracy and links improve.
