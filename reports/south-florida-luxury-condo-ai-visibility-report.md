Luxury property discovery can begin before a buyer knows a development’s name. A question about where to buy a luxury condominium in a particular city can produce an AI-generated shortlist that shapes which properties the buyer considers, how those properties are understood, and whom the buyer contacts.

Appearing in that answer is only the first step. A recommended development may be described with outdated or irrelevant information, confused with another property, or paired with a telephone number or website belonging to an independent agent, brokerage, or unofficial project site. When that destination is presented as the official sales channel, a buyer seeking the development’s sales team may instead establish the relationship elsewhere. That intermediary may introduce competing properties, changing the buyer’s consideration set before the development has an opportunity to present its own offering.

This report examines that path from discovery to inquiry: which developments appear without being named, whether AI represents them accurately, and whether it correctly identifies their official or authorized sales channels. The distinction matters because visibility alone does not establish an accurate presentation or a direct buyer relationship. The findings help developers identify where public information and AI responses may misrepresent their offering or redirect an intended direct inquiry.

**A key finding:** Google’s named comparison presented an independently operated Bahia Mar website as a developer portal, even though that site’s footer disclaims official developer status. The development appeared in the answer, but the identified sales destination belonged to a different organization. [See the Bahia Mar finding](#finding-bahia-mar).

## What this study examined

The study began with an exploratory South Florida-wide search and two named-project follow-ups. Americas Great Resorts then recorded twelve responses in the county comparison on September 10, 2026: six from ChatGPT and six from Google AI Mode. The same six county prompts covered Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties. Each county search used Firefox incognito, logged out, with a fresh session.

Three questions asked for up to five developments suitable for a full-time primary residence without naming any projects. Three subsequent questions named three developments within each county and requested a comparison of developer and brand responsibilities, hotel presence, privacy and services, construction status, expected move-in timing, and official sales websites.

The nine comparison developments were deliberately selected to examine different locations, development stages, and residential service models. They are a comparison sample, not a market census. The county charts also include fifteen other developments recommended by at least one platform.

A separate round on the same date added six responses to shorter questions about Miami, Fort Lauderdale, and Palm Beach. Its [separate market-query charts](#natural-market-queries) record all 33 developments discussed in those answers. The earlier charts retain their original results. Findings and inclusion indicators are assigned to individual questions and rounds, not carried from one answer to another.

**Scope of verification:** All three rounds were conducted on September 10, 2026, with one captured answer per question on each tested platform: nine exploratory answers, twelve county answers, and six market-query answers, totaling 27 across separate protocols. Absence means absence from that individual answer, not persistent invisibility. Differences between answers may reflect both prompt differences and ordinary answer variability; the study does not measure how often the same question would produce the same result.

This report records the captured answers and selected source checks. It does not certify every statement in those answers. Construction targets, inventory, prices, and operational arrangements can change. The findings distinguish supported facts, documented discrepancies, and assertions not fully verified.

## The initial South Florida-wide search

Before selecting the county comparison groups, the initial discovery question asked: **What are the best new luxury condominium developments and branded residences currently selling in South Florida, and why should I consider them?** It did not name a development or restrict the answer to a particular county.

That question and two follow-ups were run across Google AI, Gemini, and ChatGPT, producing nine exploratory answers. The follow-ups named Cipriani, Bentley, and Four Seasons Coconut Grove to examine project descriptions and authorized sales contacts. The regional answers included developments beyond Miami, so they did not support a claim that Broward and Palm Beach County were entirely missing.

The later county questions gave each county its own discovery test using the same primary-residence buyer need. Their twelve responses form the charts below. The earlier nine answers are a separate exploratory record, with different prompts and less complete session metadata; they are not added to the county totals. The [original three questions](#initial-test-questions) and the six county questions appear in the method section.

## Selected discrepancies from the earlier county round

<a id="finding-cipriani"></a>

### 1. Cipriani: visibility with conflicting project facts

Google recommended Cipriani in its Miami-Dade discovery answer while describing 80 stories and a 2028 estimated completion. Its named comparison repeated 80 stories but gave summer 2027 move-in. The current official project site describes 85 stories and advertises summer/Q3 2027 occupancy. ChatGPT’s named comparison used 85 stories and summer 2027. [Cipriani Residences Miami: official project site](https://ciprianiresidencesmiami.com/).

The floor count conflicts with the current official description. Google’s two answers also differ on timing. An apparent later availability date can matter to a buyer planning a move, even when the development is recommended. The official occupancy date remains an advertised target; this observation does not establish a guaranteed closing date or a lost sale.

<a id="finding-bahia-mar"></a>

### 2. Bahia Mar: an unofficial source presented as a developer portal

Google’s Broward comparison directs buyers to `bahiamarfortlauderdaleresidences.com` in a list described as developers’ dedicated property portals. That site’s footer identifies Miami Dream Properties and explicitly states that it is not the developer’s official website.

The same independent page publishes a 256-room hotel figure that also appears in Google’s answer. The current official project site describes a 200-room hotel with distinct private and resort residences. ChatGPT’s comparison used 200 rooms. [St. Regis Bahia Mar: official project site](https://srrbahiamar.com/).

This example combines a source-role error with a project-program discrepancy. The cited page and answer share the conflicting figure, although that does not prove the page was the answer’s sole source. A buyer requesting the developer’s channel is directed to an independently operated site, while the described hotel scale also changes. The issue is the identification of that channel, not the legitimacy of brokerage services.

<a id="finding-ritz"></a>

### 3. Ritz-Carlton Fort Lauderdale: a sales-link label does not match its destination

Google labels a link Douglas Elliman Development Marketing but directs it to a Manhattan Miami brokerage page. The destination describes its own brokerage perspective and separately acknowledges Douglas Elliman’s sales role.

The official project site identifies Douglas Elliman as exclusive sales and marketing partner and provides the project’s inquiry channel. [Ritz-Carlton Fort Lauderdale Beach: official project site](https://www.theresidencesfortlauderdalebeach.com/).

Naming the authorized sales organization correctly is not sufficient if the link opens a different organization’s page. This is a verified mismatch between link label and destination identity. An authorized exclusive sales brokerage is part of the development’s official sales channel; its involvement is not itself a discrepancy.

<a id="finding-waldorf"></a>

### 4. Waldorf Pompano: the gallery address becomes the building location

Google’s Broward discovery answer places the beachfront development at 1213 South Ocean Boulevard. The official site identifies that address as its sales gallery. Google’s named comparison gives the building address as 1350 South Ocean Boulevard, which is also identified in the dated construction report. [Waldorf Astoria Pompano Beach: official project site](https://waldorfastoriaresidencespompanobeach.com/); [Florida YIMBY: May 5, 2026 construction report](https://floridayimby.com/2026/05/waldorf-astoria-residences-pompano-beach-tops-out-at-1350-south-ocean-boulevard.html).

Both addresses have a legitimate role, but they are not interchangeable. The error is the attribution of the gallery address to the development itself. A valid sales contact location can still be an inaccurate description of where the buyer would live.

<a id="finding-place-labels"></a>

### 5. Place labels can conflict with an otherwise relevant answer

Google’s Broward comparison opens with Ritz-Carlton Fort Lauderdale at 551 Bayshore Drive, but later linked place labels say Naples, Florida. Waldorf Pompano narrative is paired with a later place label saying Miami, Florida. A St. Regis Bahia Mar label carries a nightly price and hotel reviews alongside narrative describing pre-construction.

ChatGPT’s Palm Beach discovery answer shows a related presentation issue: a West Palm Beach Ritz-Carlton label appears above the Palm Beach Gardens recommendation, and a Shorecrest label appears above a recommendation for Caretta. The narrative itself distinguishes the intended developments.

These inconsistencies are visible in the captured text. The rich-media rendering and all associated entity destinations have not been fully inspected, and ChatGPT’s copied link destinations were not retained. The evidence supports a presentation mismatch; it does not establish every click-through destination or identify which operating hotel supplied a price or review count.

<a id="finding-perigon"></a>

### 6. The Perigon: competing facts also exist on official public surfaces

Google’s Miami-Dade discovery answer gives 72 residences at The Perigon; ChatGPT gives 73. The current official homepage and vision page state 73, while the official news page republishes excerpts describing both 72 and 73. [The Perigon: official project site](https://theperigonmiamibeach.com/); [The Perigon: official news page](https://theperigonmiamibeach.com/news).

The chronology matters. The official news page features 72-residence excerpts linked to press files dated May 2026, while its 73-residence excerpts include coverage dated November and December 2024. The 72 figure therefore has a recent public basis on the project’s own site. This is a conflict between the homepage description and featured coverage, not evidence that Google necessarily relied on stale information. The sources alone do not establish which count governs the current offering or how either platform selected its figure.

### South Flagler House: official sources publish different residence counts

ChatGPT’s new Palm Beach answer gives 108 residences. The project residences page specifies 105, while Related Ross’s own property page lists 108. <a id="verification-limits"></a>

## Verification limits and buyer-fit judgments

Some answers go beyond describing a development and make strong claims about privacy, financial readiness, or likely buyer outcomes. The following were not established by the supplied evidence and are not presented here as verified facts:

- Google’s claim that South Flagler House ranks at the top for financial readiness and long-term resale stability.
- Assurances about protected views, association capital funding, or complete separation of residents from service and contractor movement.
- A current closing date for Ritz-Carlton Palm Beach Gardens based solely on references to initial occupancy, final construction, and an earlier spring 2026 target. Phased occupancy and ongoing work can coexist.
- Every volunteered inventory, price, fee, unit-count, and operational claim concerning the additional recommended developments, including Mandarin Oriental Boca Raton, Selene, Andare, and St. Regis Miami.

Hotel presence can influence buyer preference, but it does not by itself establish how much hotel activity reaches private residential areas. Dedicated entrances, elevators, lounges, and contractual access arrangements matter. Google’s Mr. C comparison acknowledges residential separation; that qualification should be retained when evaluating its privacy judgments.

ChatGPT also favored a shorter construction runway when interpreting a full-time primary residence, although the discovery prompt imposed no moving deadline. Its decision to mention but exclude Four Seasons Coconut Grove illustrates how an expressed buyer-fit assumption can influence a shortlist without constituting a factual error.

## Why this matters to the direct buyer relationship

The first inquiry can determine who introduces the development, explains its current offering, and maintains the next conversation with the buyer. If an AI answer labels an independent website or contact as official, that relationship may begin with a different organization than the buyer intended.

An intermediary may introduce alternatives, so the development can become one option in a broader comparison before its own sales team has spoken with the buyer. Brokerage can provide legitimate value, and authorized sales teams may themselves be brokerages. The distinction is whether the answer accurately identifies the organization receiving the inquiry.

These captures do not measure conversion, commission costs, or lost revenue. They identify points where project facts and sales-channel attribution can alter the information available to a buyer and the route of an intended direct inquiry.
