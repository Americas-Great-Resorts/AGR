## Published Article

When a prospective buyer researches a new luxury condominium development or branded residence through AI, does the answer identify the project’s official website and authorized sales team? A development can appear by name while its associated website link or sales-contact label identifies a different organization.

For developers, project marketing leaders, and exclusive sales brokerages, the concern is straightforward: someone seeking the project’s official sales team should be able to identify that team and its contact route. An independent brokerage page, a similarly named hotel, and another development’s website serve different purposes. Presenting one as the official project source creates a misleading answer. A buyer seeking your authorized sales team could instead begin an inquiry through that other website.

This article examines four documented sales-channel and website mismatches from Americas Great Resorts’ research, distinguishes them from a legitimate indirect route, and provides a short procedure for checking your own development.

## Four Documented Sales-Channel and Website Mismatches

The following cases come from AGR’s [South Florida luxury condo AI visibility report](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/south-florida-luxury-condo-ai-visibility-report/) and [ten-market luxury condo AI visibility study](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/luxury-condo-ai-visibility-study/).

### 1. An independent website presented as an official developer portal

**St. Regis Bahia Mar, Fort Lauderdale: source-role error.** In a September 10, 2026 Google AI Mode response comparing specifically named developments in Broward County, the answer presented `bahiamarfortlauderdaleresidences.com` as a developer portal. The site’s footer identified Miami Dream Properties and explicitly disclaimed official developer status.

The discrepancy concerned the authority assigned to the website: an independently operated page was presented as an official developer source despite its own disclaimer.

### 2. The sales organization named correctly, but the link opens another brokerage

**The Ritz-Carlton Residences, Fort Lauderdale Beach: label/destination mismatch.** Google AI Mode’s named Broward comparison labeled a link Douglas Elliman Development Marketing; it opened a Manhattan Miami brokerage page. The [official project website](https://www.theresidencesfortlauderdalebeach.com/) identifies Douglas Elliman as its exclusive sales and marketing partner.

The answer named the right sales organization. The link opened a different one. The Manhattan Miami page itself acknowledged Douglas Elliman’s sales role.

### 3. A residence recommendation links to a similarly named hotel

**ʻIlima, Honolulu: different property.** A ChatGPT seasonal-home answer used `ilima.com` as the Website destination. The retrieved page was the Ilima Hotel in Waikiki. Ward Village’s residence directory links ʻIlima to the shared Mohala website. Similar names referred to separate properties.

### 4. A recorded address belongs to another development, with the final destination unresolved

**Four Seasons Private Residences Coconut Grove: recorded-address mismatch.** A ChatGPT broad-discovery answer’s Website button contained `park-grove.com/one/`, associated with the separate Park Grove development. The final redirect could not be established. The recorded address supports a mismatch finding; the eventual destination remains unresolved.

### A useful counterexample: an indirect route that still provides the condominium link

**One Bennett Park, Chicago.** A ChatGPT full-time-living answer used `onebennettpark.com`, which resolved to Related Rentals’ project page. That page supplied a Luxury Condominiums link to Related’s sales site. This was an indirect developer route, rather than a demonstrated dead end or outside-broker interception.

| Case type | What the review must distinguish |
| --- | --- |
| Source-role error | Who operates the page versus the authority the answer assigns to it |
| Label/destination mismatch | The named organization versus the organization behind the linked page |
| Different property | Similar names versus the actual development identity |
| Unresolved recorded-address mismatch | The address saved in the answer versus a final destination that remains unverified |
| Legitimate indirect route | An additional navigation step versus an incorrect sales destination |

## For Branded Residences, Identify Each Organization’s Role

A branded residence can have several legitimate public presentations: the hospitality brand’s residence directory, the developer’s website, a dedicated project site, and the exclusive sales brokerage’s project page. The review should establish what each organization does and which contact handles original developer inventory.

A brokerage can be the authorized sales team. An independent broker may also legitimately represent buyers. The question is whether the answer accurately describes that relationship and provides the contact route it claims to provide.

For your own project, compare each destination with an approved record of the developer, brand relationship, authorized sales organization, and current inquiry channel. A familiar brand name or project photograph alone should not settle that comparison.

## A Short Procedure for Checking Your Development

Start with that approved project record. Then test three question types: an unnamed market search, a comparison naming your development, and a request for its official sales contact. For the contact check, use questions such as:

- Who is the official sales team for \[project\]?
- How do I contact sales for \[project\]?
- Who handles developer inventory at \[project\]?

For each answer, save the question, platform, date, and displayed link label. Open the link, record its original address and final destination separately, and identify the page operator. Mark anything unresolved rather than assigning an assumed role. Repeat the observation before treating one result as a recurring pattern.
