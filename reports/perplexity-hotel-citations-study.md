## Published Article

If you want your hotel recommended by Perplexity, appearing in the answer is only part of the concern. The description also needs to represent your property accurately. Americas Great Resorts examined 80 Perplexity hotel answers to check whether the cited pages supported the facts and whether the same hotel returned with different evidence when the question was repeated.

The searches covered six destinations. Of 2,726 factual assertion occurrences, 2,271 had at least one cited page supporting the complete statement: 83.3%. Among 45 cases where the same hotel appeared under the same discovery question twice, 37 shared no cited URL.

For hotel owners and marketing leaders, those findings create two separate checks. First, is the property appearing? Second, do the facts and supporting pages justify the description a traveler receives?

**Search date: October 6, 2026. Scope: 40 questions, repeated in two fresh sessions. Page checks: October 6–7, 2026.**

Three findings matter most:

- **A linked answer can still contain unsupported details.** The review found 272 claim/citation pairs where a readable page did not establish the attached assertion.
- **The same fact can return with different evidence.** Of 54 repeated discovery assertions with citations in both answers, 44 retained no cited destination after observed redirects were accounted for.
- **Source support and factual accuracy are different.** One cited description supported calling a hotel bar guest-only, while fuller property information distinguished daytime guest access from evening public access.

For practical steps to improve your hotel’s AI visibility, use the [hotel AI recommendation playbook](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/how-to-get-hotel-recommended-by-ai/). This research examines the descriptions and supporting evidence returned by Perplexity.

## What we tested

Each search contained 30 hotel-discovery questions and 10 questions about named hotels. All 80 answers were available for analysis, and all contained links. There were no missing answer records in this collection.

The discovery questions covered New York City, the Miami area, the Los Angeles area, Chicago, Napa Valley, and Maui. Each destination was tested for overall luxury, romance, family travel, wellness, and distinctive local character. Every question requested three hotels, concrete property facts, and supporting links.

Miami included Miami Beach and Surfside. Los Angeles included Beverly Hills. Napa Valley covered the valley destination rather than only Napa city. January 15–17, 2027 supplied a common stay scenario; live prices and room availability were not requested.

The named-hotel questions asked about operating status, location, pool, spa, and family suitability. They covered Aman New York, The Whitby Hotel, The Setai, Four Seasons Hotel at The Surf Club, The Beverly Hills Hotel, The Peninsula Chicago, Four Seasons Resort Maui at Wailea, Hotel Wailea, Auberge du Soleil, and Solage. Both question types requested links and allowed unresolved facts to remain unresolved.

We separated compound descriptions into individual factual assertions, including details beyond the requested quota. A sentence describing a pool, a spa, and a restaurant can contribute three assertions. Repeated assertions in different answers count again because each appearance presents information to a traveler.

## Do linked sources support hotel facts?

The 80 answers contained 2,726 factual assertion occurrences. Of these, 2,522 had a citation mapped to their sentence or bullet, giving 92.5% citation coverage. The remaining 204 appeared without a locally attached citation, including factual details repeated in uncited summary bullets.

A link elsewhere in the answer was not automatically assigned to those statements. This separates an answer that contains sources from an individual assertion accompanied by evidence.

Some assertions carried several citations. Assessing each attached page separately produced 2,911 claim/citation pairs across 134 distinct cited addresses.

| Result for the individual claim/citation pair | What the check means | Pairs | Share of all 2,911 pairs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supported | The cited evidence establishes the complete assertion | 2,419 | 83.1% |
| Partial | It establishes a narrower statement or misses a material qualification | 46 | 1.6% |
| Unsupported | Readable evidence does not establish the assertion | 272 | 9.3% |
| Contradicted | Readable evidence explicitly conflicts with the assertion | 7 | 0.2% |
| Unverifiable | Access or capture limitations prevent a defensible judgment | 167 | 5.7% |
| **Total** |  | **2,911** | **100%** |

*Percentages are rounded. Excluding the 167 unverifiable pairs leaves 2,744 assessable pairs; 2,419 of those, or 88.2%, were supported. Unavailable evidence remains outside that narrower denominator.*

An unsupported citation does not necessarily make its attached fact false. Another page may establish it. Conversely, a readable source can repeat an incomplete description.

At the assertion level, 2,271 of 2,726 had at least one fully supporting citation. That 83.3% figure includes uncited assertions in the denominator. No additional assertion was upgraded solely by combining several partial citations. Seven assertions had an explicitly conflicting cited source; three of those also had a supporting source. Those disagreements were retained rather than erased.

### Discovery and named-hotel answers behaved differently

| Measure | Discovery answers | Named-hotel answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answers reviewed | 60 | 20 |
| Factual assertion occurrences | 2,198 | 528 |
| Assertions with mapped citations | 1,994 of 2,198 (90.7%) | 528 of 528 (100%) |
| Assertions with at least one fully supporting citation | 1,806 of 2,198 (82.2%) | 465 of 528 (88.1%) |
| Individual claim/citation pairs | 2,149 | 762 |
| Fully supported pairs | 1,877 of 2,149 (87.3%) | 542 of 762 (71.1%) |

The named-hotel answers had better assertion-level coverage but lower support per individual citation. Those results can coexist: attaching several sources may give an assertion one good supporting page alongside other pages that do not establish it. More citations are not the same as more corroboration.

### Correct facts and supporting citations are different

We also checked factual status against relevant property and brand information, separately from whether the attached citation supported the wording. That review confirmed 1,279 assertions, contradicted three, and left 1,444 unresolved.

“Unresolved” means the available authoritative evidence did not settle the complete assertion. It does not mean the assertion was wrong. Likewise, the three contradicted assertions are confirmed conflicts found in this review, not an estimate of the total number of errors.

| Citation result for the assertion | Fact confirmed | Fact contradicted | Fact unresolved | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| At least one fully supporting citation | 1,248 | 1 | 1,022 | 2,271 |
| Partial support, without a fully supporting citation | 3 | 1 | 25 | 29 |
| Readable citations did not support it | 10 | 0 | 78 | 88 |
| Contradictory citation, without supporting or partial evidence | 0 | 1 | 3 | 4 |
| Remaining citation assessment unverifiable | 1 | 0 | 129 | 130 |
| No locally mapped citation | 17 | 0 | 187 | 204 |
| **Total** | **1,279** | **3** | **1,444** | **2,726** |

The practical distinction appears in both directions. Ten assertions were independently confirmed even though their readable citations did not support them. One assertion had supporting cited wording but conflicted with the property’s fuller access information. The 83.3% support figure therefore cannot be presented as an accuracy score.

*Citation columns compare the addresses recorded in the answers. A case is the same hotel under the same question in both searches. A property can contribute several cases. A shared website means a shared hostname, not necessarily the same page.*

**New York City** produced the widest range, with 17 properties. Its three repeated hotel/question cases were Baccarat for overall luxury and The Carlyle and The Greenwich Hotel for couples. None retained a cited page or website.

**The Miami area** retained its overall-luxury trio of The Setai, The Surf Club, and Faena, while the complete citation set changed for each hotel.

**The Los Angeles area** had nine matched hotel/question cases, yet none retained a source website. Hotel Bel-Air moved from a travel guide to its own property page. This illustrates source change within the observed answers even where the property remained recommended.

**Chicago** retained a URL for The Peninsula Chicago in the overall-luxury question, but added official and travel-listing pages changed the complete evidence set.

**Napa Valley** retained Auberge du Soleil, Bardessono, and Stanly Ranch for local character. None of those three entries retained its cited address. Across Napa’s nine matched cases, five retained a website, but only two retained a URL, both involving Solage under different questions.

**Maui** drew from seven properties. All three URL-retaining cases involved Four Seasons Resort Maui at Wailea, under overall luxury, couples, and wellness. Its family answer retained the hotel and website while changing every cited page.

## Do citations change when the question stays the same?

Of the 45 matched discovery cases, eight shared at least one cited URL and 37 shared none. None retained its complete URL set unchanged.

The ten named-hotel comparisons were more stable: nine retained a URL. Their complete citation sets still changed in every case.

Hotel disappearance is separate. The first discovery search contained 90 recommendation positions; 45 hotel/question appearances carried into the second search and 45 did not. Those absent properties cannot contribute a same-hotel citation comparison.

### Pages versus websites

Sixteen discovery cases retained a website, but only eight retained a URL. The other eight kept a familiar website while changing every linked address. An amenities page and a homepage can belong to the same hotel while providing different evidence.

To measure how much evidence overlapped, we compared shared items with all distinct items across each pair of answers. Zero means no overlap; 100% means identical sets. We averaged hotel comparisons within each question before averaging the eligible questions.

| Average overlap measure | Discovery: 45 cases across 25 questions | Named hotels: 10 cases across 10 questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cited page addresses as recorded | 4.4% | 17.4% |
| Website hostnames as recorded | 14.5% | 24.8% |
| Page destinations after observed redirects/canonical addresses | 5.6% | 19.8% |
| Website hostnames after those observed changes | 15.3% | 26.1% |
| Same complete assertion attached to the same destination | 0.6% | 7.3% |

*Five discovery questions had no hotel in common and are excluded from these same-hotel averages. Where a destination change could not be observed, the recorded address was retained. The last row also reflects changes in the factual details included, so it is not a measure of citation change alone.*

### Stable facts with changing sources

We therefore examined assertions repeated in both answers separately. Matching preserved meaningful conditions: a general pool count was not treated as identical to a statement adding saltwater, heating, or an age restriction.

| Repeated-assertion comparison | Discovery | Named hotels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same hotel, question, and complete assertion in both answers | 62 | 83 |
| Those assertions with mapped citations in both answers | 54 | 83 |
| Retained at least one cited destination | 10 | 46 |
| Retained no cited destination | 44 | 37 |

These counts isolate changing evidence from changing factual content. A hotel’s pool or spa could remain in the description while the pages attached to that assertion changed. That is a reason to inspect a repeat answer, even when the property name and main selling points look familiar.

## What the source checks found at individual hotels

### The Setai: a specific page resolved missing information

The first named-hotel answer cited The Setai’s homepage but left its spa and family suitability unresolved. The second linked to the Our Hotel page and named Valmont Spa and three pools, including a designated family pool.

The checked page supports those details: a 75-degree lap pool, an 85-degree center pool, and a 90-degree family pool. The homepage body copy emphasizes the property’s identity without describing those pool features.

The improvement was specific. The second answer supplied evidence for amenities the first had not established. A family pool still does not establish every policy affecting children, but it gives the traveler a concrete fact instead of unresolved general suitability.

### Aman New York: the linked page did not establish the named package

In the second New York couples answer, Perplexity attributed an “Ultimate Indulgence After Hours” experience to Aman New York. Its description included a private three-hour couples experience with garden soaking, steam, massage, and refreshments. The attached citation led to Aman’s wellness page.

That page establishes substantial wellness facilities, including three floors devoted to wellness and a 20-meter (65-foot) heated indoor swimming pool. It did not establish the named package and bundled inclusions.

The package name and those inclusions appeared on the official spa page for Auberge du Soleil in Napa Valley. The same Perplexity search also described that package under Auberge du Soleil in its Napa couples answer.

This establishes a concrete support problem: the Aman citation did not substantiate the specific experience attributed to Aman. It does not establish how the answer was generated or prove that no similar private experience could ever be arranged. A traveler should not be given that package as an established Aman offering on this evidence.

### Beverly Wilshire: evidence for another hotel did not transfer with the brand

The first Los Angeles family answer used a Chicago family-hotel guide to support a statement about Four Seasons family programming under Beverly Wilshire.

The guide’s recognition applied to Four Seasons Hotel Chicago. It did not establish the asserted programming for Beverly Wilshire or for every Four Seasons property.

The second answer offered more relevant evidence: the Beverly Wilshire property page described family accommodation and a private macaron-making experience with a junior apron. Those details addressed the named hotel.

The lesson is property identity. Even a credible source about the correct brand can be the wrong evidence for the hotel in the answer.

### The Portrait Bar: supported wording lost an access condition

The first New York luxury answer described The Fifth Avenue Hotel’s Portrait Bar as guest-only. Its cited hotel list used that wording, so the citation supported the abbreviated description.

The property’s dining listing with The Leading Hotels of the World lists daytime hours of 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. separately from evening hours open to the public. The unqualified guest-only description does not preserve that public evening access.

This is the difference between faithful repetition and an accurate account of access. A hotel guest could infer an exclusive evening experience; a local visitor could assume the bar was unavailable entirely. The complete rule supports neither assumption.

### Baccarat: changing sources did not automatically weaken the description

Baccarat’s New York overall-luxury citations changed from one editorial list to four official pages covering the hotel, FAQ, pool, and spa.

Both answers described a pool and Spa de La Mer, amenities supported by the checked sources. The official pool page also established the heated indoor pool described in the second answer. Complete source replacement changed the evidence to inspect without demonstrating declining accuracy.

## What hotel teams should check

Start with details that affect a booking decision: operating status, location, family restrictions, pool access, spa facilities, dining access, and package inclusions.

**Save the exact statement and attached page.** Keep the hotel, question, answer date, and citation together. A link elsewhere in the answer may support another detail.

**Confirm the property before the amenity.** Check that the page discusses the right hotel and facility. The Beverly Wilshire and Aman examples show why a recognizable brand or relevant-looking spa page is insufficient.

**Preserve the conditions.** Check ages, hours, fees, room categories, and reservation requirements. Guest-only, complimentary, and adults-only can become misleading when their conditions disappear.

**Separate the correction needed.** If the cited page is accurate but the answer misstates it, adding more general website copy may not address the problem. If the source itself is incomplete, correct the public description. If the page cannot be assessed, preserve that uncertainty.

**Repeat the same question and inspect both facts and sources.** A retained property can acquire a different description. A retained fact can acquire different evidence. A retained URL can continue to carry the same omission.

## Scope and limits of this review

This is a dated comparison of two Perplexity searches, with 60 discovery answers and 20 named-hotel answers. The prompts explicitly requested citations. The results describe this collection and should not be generalized to every Perplexity search or every luxury hotel.

The analysis preserves hotel identity and material qualifiers, counts repeated factual assertions as occurrences, and deduplicates repeated links within a hotel entry for set comparisons. An unavailable page is kept separate from readable evidence that fails to support a statement. Live page checks cannot establish precisely which version Perplexity accessed.

The claim classifications were produced with AI and have not undergone independent human double-coding. Factual status remains unresolved where authoritative evidence did not settle the assertion. The results are editorial audit findings, with the underlying records retained for correction and review, rather than a platform-wide accuracy certification.

This collection is separate from the earlier AGR [research across ten destinations](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-recommendations-research/). It does not add Perplexity observations to that earlier dataset or make a controlled comparison with another platform.

## Questions hotel teams ask about Perplexity citations

### Is an uncited statement automatically false?

No. This review independently confirmed some uncited assertions. The missing local citation is an evidence gap for the reader, not proof of a factual error.

### Are changing sources a bad sign?

They call for inspection. New pages may supply more specific support, while repeated pages may preserve an incomplete description. Source change alone does not establish improvement or deterioration.

### If Perplexity names the same hotel twice, is the evidence the same?

Often it was not in this collection. Among 45 cases where the same hotel appeared under the same discovery question twice, 37 shared no cited URL. None retained its complete URL set unchanged.

## Review the evidence behind your hotel’s visibility

The useful outcome is a more precise correction list: a claim attached to the wrong property, an incomplete access rule, a missing amenity detail, or a source that needs another check.

The same discipline applies across AI platforms: compare what the answer says, what any cited page establishes, and what is true at the property.
