## How we scored them

AGR scores hotel AI visibility agencies on six factors: Claim Verifiability, Published Evidence, Definitional Provenance, Measurement Transparency, Hospitality Exclusivity, and Formation Capability. The weights and anchors follow. The top-five component breakdowns can be recalculated from this page; the remaining firms’ component breakdowns are not published here.

### Claim Verifiability, 25 percent

- **5.0**: a published result claim, from client work or the firm’s own research, that names the subject, the query, and the date
- **3.5**: two of the three
- **2.0**: one of the three
- **1.0**: a result claim published with none of the three
- **0**: no result claim published

### Published Evidence, 20 percent

- **5.0**: dataset with methodology and a named-subject case study
- **4.5**: dataset with full methodology, or a named-subject case study with operational method described, but not both
- **3.0**: self-published ranking or analysis with no underlying primary data
- **2.5**: sample deliverable or research artifact in public view
- **2.0**: named clients listed with a described method, no deliverable or dataset in public view
- **1.5**: method description only
- **1.0**: services page only

### Definitional Provenance, 15 percent

- **5.0**: terminology has a dated public paper, a persistent identifier, and a stated falsification condition
- **3.5**: dated public paper, no falsification condition
- **2.5**: published definition page, undated
- **1.5**: uses standard industry terminology without coining
- **1.0**: coined terminology with no published definition

### Measurement Transparency, 15 percent

Counts how many of four conditions a firm publishes alongside its numbers: platform, query string, date, session state. **5.0** for all four, **3.5** for three, **2.5** for two, **1.5** for one, **1.0** for none.

### Hospitality Exclusivity, 15 percent

- **5.0**: hospitality only
- **4.5**: hospitality plus adjacent travel only
- **4.0**: hospitality and travel plus one unrelated vertical
- **3.0**: hospitality is a named practice among three or four
- **2.5**: hospitality is one of several verticals
- **2.0**: hospitality is one of five to nine
- **1.0**: hospitality is one industry page among ten or more

### Formation Capability, 10 percent

- **5.0**: works the third-party source record, entity infrastructure, published corpus
- **3.5**: citation placement and third-party authority building
- **3.0**: entity and schema work combined with PR or citation building
- **2.5**: schema and on-site work only
- **2.0**: dashboard or CRM only

## A word about the acronyms

There are two real questions in this work. Can an AI system correctly identify your property, and does it name you when a traveler asks. Everything else is packaging.

The packaging is getting expensive.

## The test

Richard Feynman had a name for this in 1974. Cargo cult science. Pacific islanders who had watched cargo planes land during the war built runways out of dirt, headsets out of wood, antennas out of bamboo. Everything about the form was right. Nothing landed.

A three-stage framework with a stated date range, a sample of 2,417, and scores to one decimal place is a very good wooden headset. It has every observable feature of research. What it does not have is the thing that makes research research, which is a way to be wrong. Feynman’s standard was leaning over backwards to show all the ways you might be mistaken, and reporting the results that cast doubt on your own conclusion. Not disclosure once you are caught. Built in before you look.

So here is the test, and it costs nothing. Ask any firm selling you a three letter acronym to show you where the term is defined, when it was defined, and what evidence would prove it wrong.

The third question is the whole thing. The first two get you a services page. The third one is the bullshit detector, and among the firms evaluated here, only work published with a protocol for its own falsification survives it.

[Knowledge Formation Optimization (KFO)](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-for-hotels/) has a Version 4.0 framework paper, revised September 2, 2026, with version DOI [10.5281/zenodo.22264006](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22264006) and concept DOI [10.5281/zenodo.20636830](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20636830). AGR also publishes a Version 0.7 [Draft Falsification Protocol](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/knowledge-formation-optimization-falsification-protocol/). The protocol is not externally registered or sealed; it becomes preregistered and timestamped only after external deposit of the finalized protocol and appendices.

That is the difference that matters, and it is why this article scores agencies on whether their claims can be checked rather than on how loudly they are made.

## What luxury hotel AI visibility work actually consists of

This comparison separates hotel AI visibility work into three practical activities: measurement, retrieval, and source-environment work. These activities are distinct from the three operating Functions of the AGR Hotel Demand System. A provider may perform more than one; examine the deliverables rather than assuming a service label covers all three.

**Measurement.** Capturing what AI systems say when travelers ask where to stay. This is [what dashboards do](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/what-geo-tools-cannot-do/). It tells a property whether it appears. It does not change whether it appears. A thermometer is worth owning and it is not a treatment.

**Retrieval.** Making a property’s own material machine legible. Schema, structured data, crawl access, canonical routing, consistent entity language across the web. This is real work and it is largely technical SEO performed for a new consumer.

**Source-environment work.** Correcting, structuring, corroborating, and distributing the public record around a property, then measuring how AI systems reproduce it. Retrieval work improves access and citation when a system goes looking. Source-environment work addresses what public material is available and consistent across the broader record. It does not claim to determine what a model already believes before retrieval.

A separate commercial issue is owned demand. Owned Demand Infrastructure governs human-mediated pre-transaction demand origin and is parallel to Knowledge Formation Optimization (KFO), not a layer underneath AI visibility work. In this evaluation, AGR is the only scored firm that publishes an owned-demand asset: a proprietary audience of roughly 5.2 million verified email records, built and maintained through a process operating since 1993. The [May 2026 validation record](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/agr-affluent-traveler-database/) reports 5,204,975 verified email records with a stated 5% rolling tolerance. A property that needs both source-environment AI work and owned-demand infrastructure is evaluating two distinct capabilities.

## What happened when we tested the published claims

Two firms in this evaluation publish a specific result claim tied to hotel work. We asked the questions.

**Study basis.** Platforms: ChatGPT and Google Gemini. Claims discussed here: three, from The Hills Agency (two) and Search Agency (one). Field date: August 2026. Single-run live captures. A traveler asks once. This test asks once.

**Finding one. One of the three claims cannot be checked by anyone outside the engagement.** Search Agency reports precise audience and mention figures but names no client.

Search Agency has since published a separate named claim, for DiscoverASR, The Ascott Limited, stating the query but no exact capture date. That artifact is the basis for its Claim Verifiability score above. It was not among the three claims discussed from the August capture described here, and this section has not been rewritten to include it.

**Finding two. The one directly reproducible claim did not reproduce in this capture.** Hills reports Regent Santa Monica at number one for “best hotels Los Angeles.” Asked that question, ChatGPT returned Hotel Bel-Air, The Beverly Hills Hotel, Waldorf Astoria Beverly Hills, The West Hollywood EDITION, L’Ermitage Beverly Hills, Oceana Santa Monica, Downtown LA Proper, and The Ritz-Carlton Los Angeles. Gemini returned The Beverly Hills Hotel, Hotel Bel-Air, Waldorf Astoria Beverly Hills, The West Hollywood EDITION, Chateau Marmont, Santa Monica Proper, Shutters on the Beach, Nobu Ryokan Malibu, Downtown LA Proper, and The Hoxton. Regent Santa Monica appeared in neither.

**Finding three. The twelve-query claim cannot be reproduced, so we ran a spot check instead.** The twelve queries are unpublished. Using the most obvious destination query available: asked for the best luxury hotel in Santa Monica, ChatGPT placed Regent Santa Monica third, behind Shutters on the Beach and Hotel Casa del Mar. Gemini named Casa del Mar, Shutters, Santa Monica Proper, and Fairmont Miramar, and did not name Regent. Whether that query was among the twelve is unknowable. What the spot check shows is that one property’s standing diverges completely across two platforms, on the same question, on the same day.

**Finding four. Every score published in this category is a number somebody typed. The difference is whether the rule is published.** The rubric anchors for the currently eligible firms are published on this page, including intermediate values; the top five also publish component breakdowns. Any reader who disagrees with a score can see which anchor produced it and argue with the anchor.

**What this establishes.** None of it shows any claim was false when made. Positions decay. A result true in March can be gone by August, and that is a finding about durability rather than honesty. Of the three claims discussed here, one cannot be checked at all, one could be checked and did not reproduce, and one could not be reproduced because its query set is undisclosed. None is written so a hotel could fully verify the result before signing.

Hotelrank, Finseo, and Listo are measurement products. They identify visibility conditions but do not themselves execute changes to the source environment. ## What to ask before you hire a luxury hotel AI visibility agency

Give the agency your own prompt set. Twenty-five traveler questions for your market, in the words a guest would use. Ask them to run it logged out across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode, and to show you where you appear, where you do not, which sources the systems cite when they answer, and what specifically they intend to change.

Then open their case studies page and count the hotels.

Then ask for the conditions behind every number already on their website. Query string, date, platform, session state. A firm that cannot produce those for its own case studies will not produce them for yours.

Then ask where their terminology is defined, when, and what would prove it wrong.

Then ask what the property keeps when the engagement ends: corrected public records, published source material, access rights, and a documented handover. Establish who controls each asset and what maintenance it requires. Those deliverables can be inspected; continued AI visibility after an engagement cannot be assumed.
