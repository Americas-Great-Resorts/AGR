**Americas Great Resorts | AI responses collected September 28, 2026**

**Half of the 136 developments in AGR’s research reference set received no recommendation in the broad discovery answers. More specific buyer questions expanded coverage, while selected answers revealed errors in project descriptions and website destinations.**

When a prospective buyer asks an AI assistant which luxury condominiums to consider, the answer creates a shortlist. For a development team, three questions follow: Does the project appear? Is it described accurately? Does the accompanying website lead to the right property?

Americas Great Resorts examined 900 responses from ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot across ten U.S. markets. After excluding four non-substantive responses, the study assessed 896 answers against a separately researched reference set of developments.

In the broad discovery round, **68 of 136 included developments were recommended at least once; 68 were not**. When the analysis also included more specific questions that each development demonstrably fit, coverage increased to **99 of 136, or 72.8%**.

These are results from a defined set of questions, properties and captures. They do not establish permanent AI invisibility or represent every luxury condominium development in the United States.

The research extends the discovery, accuracy and website-identification questions examined in AGR’s earlier [South Florida Luxury Condo AI Visibility Report](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/south-florida-luxury-condo-ai-visibility-report/), using a larger geographic scope and repeated questions.

## Half the reference set received a broad-discovery recommendation

The broad question asked:

> Which new luxury condo developments and branded residences are my best options in [MARKET]?

Each market had nine answers to this question: three platforms, with three repetitions each. The question named no development, imposed no fixed shortlist length and did not explicitly request citations.

Across those 90 answers, 68 of the 136 included developments appeared as recommendations at least once.

| Market | Included developments | Recommended in broad discovery | Not recommended in broad discovery | Recommended across all applicable questions |

| Miami-Dade County | 42 | 8 | 34 | 22 |
| Broward County | 13 | 7 | 6 | 11 |
| Palm Beach County | 10 | 9 | 1 | 10 |

The 50.0% broad-discovery figure gives each included development equal weight. Giving each market equal weight instead produces an average market coverage rate of 72.6%. These answer different questions: how many developments were reached, versus how much of the reference set was reached in the average selected market.

Miami-Dade and New York accounted for 56 of the 68 developments absent from broad discovery. They also had the largest reference sets. The table therefore should not be read as a ranking of markets’ overall AI information quality. Likewise, coverage of all three included Los Angeles-area developments does not establish coverage of that entire market.

A shortlist is selective by design. Coverage can vary with the number of recommendations per answer and how often the same projects recur. This study measures which included projects entered those shortlists; it does not isolate the causes of omission.

## More specific questions expanded the consideration set

A buyer seeking a seasonal residence may receive different recommendations from someone seeking full-time living, walkability or immediate occupancy.

Across broad discovery and the more specific questions each project demonstrably fit, **99 of 136 developments received at least one recommendation**. That added 31 developments to the set reached through broad discovery.

Question fit matters. An inland development was not counted as absent from an oceanfront answer. A project without sufficiently supported delivery timing was not assigned an absence from the near-term move-in question. Uncertain eligibility or question fit remained outside the relevant scoring denominator.

Three figures distinguish overall recommendation presence from supported buyer fit:

| Measure | Developments |
| --- | --- |
| Recommended somewhere in the assessed answers for their market, regardless of question fit | **111 of 136 — 81.6%** |
| Recommended in at least one question with independently established applicability | **99 of 136 — 72.8%** |
| No recommendation anywhere in the assessed answers for their market | **25 of 136 — 18.4%** |

The remaining 12 developments were recommended, but only in questions where their fit was uncertain or inapplicable. They therefore could not be counted as applicable recommendations under the study rules. Calling all 37 developments without an applicable recommendation “never mentioned” would misstate the findings. Of the 25 with no recommendation, 24 had no recorded mention; Lofty Brickell appeared without being recommended. The named list below separates these outcomes.

Across 89 developments, the coding also preserved 511 recommendation appearances outside the applicability-qualified results: 490 where question fit was uncertain and 21 where it was inapplicable. These are repeated appearances across answers, not 511 additional developments or responses. They cannot simply be added to the scored results without changing the corresponding denominator.

## Question-by-question results across all ten markets

Each market contains the ten exact questions, with three saved runs from each platform. The charts show the first five distinct recommended developments or options in each answer, or fewer when fewer were returned. Names are standardized where identity is clear. These are condensed results, not full answer transcripts.

Entries follow the first substantive shortlist, table or project discussion, including an opening recommendation summary where present. Repeated names count once; grouped projects are separated in their stated order. Numbers show presentation order, not an AGR ranking. Additional options are marked.

### Source legend

**O** = Official project website · **A** = Authorized sales website · **B** = Broker/advisor · **T** = Third-party publication, listing or review site · **U** = Unverified · **X** = Different property · **R** = Related rental page · **—** = No attributable source recorded.

Source codes summarize websites, map-card URLs and citations associated with the property in the saved answer. An O does not necessarily mean an official Website button was displayed. Multiple codes indicate multiple source types. Unverified sites are not assumed to be brokers or mirrors. A dash means no association was established in the saved data; it does not prove the original interface had no link. † marks an additional option. Blank rows mean fewer than five properties were returned; nonanswers remain excluded from scoring.

The charts include uncertain, excluded and outside-reference properties or businesses when the AI recommended them. They do not certify current inventory or buyer suitability. The 136-development scoring and headline figures use the full assessed answers and established eligibility rules, not only the five displayed entries.

## The platforms recommended different sets of developments

Returning more developments does not, by itself, establish a better answer. Accuracy, current inventory, buyer fit and website destinations are separate considerations. Differences in answer length and repeated selections can affect these project-reached counts. The collection also did not establish equivalent model versions, product modes or account conditions across platforms.

| Platform | Developments recommended in broad discovery | Developments recommended across all applicable questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | 62 of 136 — 45.6% | 93 of 136 — 68.4% |
| Gemini | 46 of 136 — 33.8% | 72 of 136 — 52.9% |
| Microsoft Copilot | 45 of 136 — 33.1% | 70 of 136 — 51.5% |

Sixty developments received an applicable recommendation on all three platforms. Twenty-three received one on only a single platform. The platform counts overlap and cannot be added together.

For a development team, this variation makes a single answer from a single platform an incomplete visibility check.

## Being recommended did not always produce the right website destination

The website review distinguished a source citation from a Website button accompanying a recommendation. It also distinguished the address recorded in an answer from the final page retrieved during subsequent checks.

Three examples illustrate different problems:

**Four Seasons Private Residences Coconut Grove: a different project’s address.** In one ChatGPT broad-discovery answer, the saved Website button beneath the Four Seasons recommendation contained the address park-grove.com/one/. The [official Four Seasons residence page](https://www.fourseasons.com/residences/private_residences/coconut-grove/) links to the Coconut Grove project, while [Related’s Park Grove page](https://relatedgroup.com/properties/park-grove/) identifies the separate Park Grove development. The recorded address was a mismatch; the final redirect from that captured link could not be established.

**ʻIlima in Honolulu: a similarly named hotel.** A ChatGPT seasonal-home recommendation used ilima.com as its Website destination. The retrieved page was the [Ilima Hotel in Waikiki](https://ilima.com/). [Ward Village’s residence directory](https://www.wardvillage.com/residences/) links both its ʻIlima and Melia residences to the shared Mohala website. The similar names referred to different properties.

**One Bennett Park in Chicago: a rental-first route with a condominium link.** A ChatGPT full-time-living recommendation used onebennettpark.com, which resolved to [Related Rentals’ One Bennett Park page](https://www.relatedrentals.com/apartment-rentals/chicago/streeterville/one-bennett-park). That page also provides a “Luxury Condominiums” link to Related’s sales site. This was an indirect route through the developer’s rental presentation, not evidence of a dead end or an outside broker intercepting a buyer.

These selected examples do not establish an overall rate of incorrect destinations. Initial website checks were recorded on September 29, 2026 (UTC), after response collection; redirects can change. The study did not observe buyer clicks, inquiries, transactions, diverted leads or commissions.

## Accurate project descriptions require a separate check

Recommendation presence did not establish factual accuracy.

A Copilot answer placed **Aman New York Residences in Tribeca**. [Aman’s official location information](https://www.aman.com/hotels/aman-new-york) places the property in Midtown’s Crown Building at Fifth Avenue and 57th Street.

Another Copilot answer described **Park Lane Ala Moana as part of Howard Hughes’ Ward Village**. [MacNaughton’s project record](https://www.macnaughton.com/portfolio/park-lane) identifies Park Lane at Ala Moana Center and its MacNaughton/Kobayashi development partnership. [Ward Village’s residence directory](https://www.wardvillage.com/residences/) provides a separate account of that community’s projects. The distinction rests on the documented location and development identity, not simply the absence of a name from a directory.

A ChatGPT move-in answer contained a calendar contradiction: it set a September 2027 cutoff, described Cormont’s Tower One delivery as the end of 2027 and treated that delivery as inside the requested window. This identifies an inconsistency within the answer; it does not independently verify the project’s actual completion schedule.

The accuracy review could examine recommendations outside the included reference set, as with Aman and Park Lane. Those examples were not added to the 136-development denominator. These targeted checks illustrate material errors without establishing a platform-wide factual error rate or certifying every other statement.

## How developments qualified, and what the results can establish

The research screened luxury whole-unit condominiums and branded residences with evidence of active original developer inventory. Preconstruction, under-construction and completed developments with remaining developer inventory could qualify. There was no mandatory price floor or automatic age cutoff: “new” concerned the developer offering, rather than a uniform construction date. The prompts did not supply that operational definition, so the platforms may have interpreted “new” differently.

Ordinary searches and project, developer, brand and supporting sources informed the reference set. A live website, inquiry form, historical launch announcement, broker luxury label or resale listing alone did not establish current developer inventory.

The approved research boundaries were:

- Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach counties, separately.
 
- All Collier County for Naples–Marco Island.
 
- All five New York City boroughs.
 
- The cities of Boston and Chicago, separately.
 
- Los Angeles, Beverly Hills and West Hollywood.
 
- The 2020 Urban Honolulu census-designated place, rather than all Oʻahu.
 
- Summit and Wasatch counties for Park City–Deer Valley.

The 255-candidate reference was not an exhaustive market census. The 100 uncertain candidates stayed outside the denominator, and unresolved inventory evidence could affect which developments qualified. Of those 100 candidates, 73 received at least one recommendation in the assessed answers for their market. Their exclusion from scoring reflects unresolved eligibility, not AI absence. Additional developments appearing in AI answers, including One Park Grove, were recorded separately; appearing outside the reference set did not automatically make a project ineligible.

The retained responses were collected on September 28, 2026. All 900 responses were preserved, with four nonanswers excluded without replacement. The questions requested English and U.S. country settings and supplied no conversation history.

Three submissions per question do not prove three independent user sessions. Session isolation, personalization, account state and cache independence were not independently verified. ChatGPT’s requested web-search setting was false, although returned metadata reported web search in 297 of 300 records. These captures therefore document the provider-mediated outputs, not a controlled comparison of identical consumer-product settings.

The original protocol called for completing and freezing the reference before capture. Collection occurred first; the reference and question-applicability records were frozen at approximately 8:25 p.m. Eastern that evening, before captured answer content was reviewed. Keeping answer content separate from reference development did not erase that sequencing deviation.

Coding counted a development when an answer recommended it or presented it as an additional option. Incidental mentions, source-only references and ambiguous appearances were kept separate. Repeated references to the same project within one answer counted once. Results reflect AGR’s initial coding and targeted checks; they have not received a complete independent audit of the underlying answers and coding.

The findings describe this reference set, collection window, question wording and access method. They do not measure permanent invisibility, explain the platforms’ internal selection mechanisms or establish lost sales.

## What development teams can do with these findings

**Test discovery through the questions a buyer would actually ask.** Include broad market searches and narrower needs the development can substantiate. Record differences across platforms and repeated answers. A satisfactory response containing the project’s name does not establish that an unnamed search will surface it.

**Keep the public project record specific and current.** Clearly distinguish the condominium offering from a similarly named hotel, rental building or neighboring development. Maintain dated information about location, developer inventory, services, delivery expectations and the authorized sales organization. These records also support the positioning and sales materials discussed in AGR’s [Luxury Condo Marketing Guide for Developers](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/luxury-condo-marketing-guide/).

**Check the destination alongside the recommendation.** Inspect the actual link and any redirect. Determine whether it identifies the intended project and offers an appropriate path to its sales team. A brokerage domain may belong to an authorized sales organization; ownership and authorization require checking.

**Evaluate discovery, accuracy and website identification separately.** A project can improve on one measure while remaining weak on another. Counting mentions alone would have missed the distinctions documented here.

AGR’s [Knowledge Formation Optimization framework](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/kfo-knowledge-formation-optimization/) addresses the public information from which AI systems can describe and distinguish entities. Its practical focus includes clear project definitions, consistent official sources, corroboration and repeated measurement of observable answers. It does not control proprietary AI systems or guarantee recommendations.

Americas Great Resorts offers a 120-day [AI visibility program for new luxury condo developments and branded residences](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-visibility-condo-developments-branded-residences/). This publisher-authored study documents observed recommendation behavior and selected errors. It does not test the effectiveness of that program or demonstrate that KFO caused an improvement in visibility, inquiries or sales.
