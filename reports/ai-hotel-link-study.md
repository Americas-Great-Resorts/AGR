## Quick Answers

### Do AI hotel recommendations link to hotel websites?

Usually not. In AGR’s November 11, 2026 study, 536 of 899 answers that named at least one hotel (60%) contained no link to any hotel or hotel-brand website, either among the citations or in the answer text. Counting citations only, the figure was 705 of 899 (78%). In this collection, Google AI Mode answers were the most likely to carry a hotel website link and Gemini answers the least likely, although 96% of Google AI Mode answers cited Google’s own pages.

### How can widespread AI use coexist with low measured referral traffic?

Industry research reports that 56% of active US travelers used AI on at least one trip in the prior 12 months, while AI referrals remain a small share of the traffic measured at large travel companies. This study does not measure traffic. It documents one structural difference: AI answers can name a hotel without containing a link to the hotel’s website. Most answers in this study linked to Google, Tripadvisor, publishers or nothing at all.

### What should a hotel check first?

Its Google business information and its Tripadvisor listing. Google AI Mode presented hotels in cards inside Google’s interface, and the hotel’s website appeared in the card data for 1,303 of 1,309 cards. Tripadvisor was the recorded provider for 95% of the pins on ChatGPT’s hotel maps. The study did not test how a change to either record affects an AI answer.

### Does the same question return the same hotels across three runs?

Rarely. Each question was asked three times on each engine on the same day. In 292 of 299 complete question-and-engine groups (97.7%), at least one hotel was added or removed across the three answers. That does not mean the entire list changed.

### Do the same hotels keep leading?

Some do. The Peninsula Chicago, Halekulani, Hotel Bel-Air and Mandarin Oriental, New York led their destinations in AGR’s September 27, 2026 study, which pooled four engines, and led or co-led again in this three-engine November sample. In the other six destinations, the leader changed.

**Scope:** These findings describe one November 11, 2026 collection across 10 destinations, 100 questions and three logged-out consumer AI experiences. They measure hotels named and links present in the saved answers. They do not measure clicks, website traffic, bookings or the causes of any recommendation.

## At a glance

---

1. **AI answers named hotels and rarely linked to hotel websites.** 60% of answers that named a hotel had no hotel website link anywhere in the answer. 78% had no hotel website among their citations.
2. **Google AI Mode cited Google.** 95.7% of its citation links pointed to Google pages. Its hotel cards held the hotel’s website in their data, and no card’s link field pointed to a hotel website. More than half its answers did include a hotel website link in the answer text.
3. **ChatGPT cited publishers and Tripadvisor.** Those two destinations received 68.3% of its citation links. Tripadvisor was the recorded provider for 95.0% of its hotel map pins.
4. **Gemini often gave no sources.** 34.3% of its answers carried no citations. When it cited, 62.4% of links went to publishers and guides.
5. **Hotel lists moved between runs, and six destination leaders changed.** At least one hotel changed across same-day repeats in 292 of 299 complete groups. Four of 10 September leaders led or co-led again in November.

## What this study measures

---

Hotel marketers usually judge AI by the referral traffic it sends. That counts only the travelers who clicked a link that happened to point at the hotel. Before any click, the AI answer names hotels and attaches links: citations to sources, links in the answer text, map pins and hotel cards. Those links shape what a traveler can check directly from the answer and where an immediate click leads.

AGR asked ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Gemini the same 100 hotel questions used in its [September 27, 2026 ten-destination study](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-recommendations-research/), three times each, and recorded the hotels named and the links present in each saved answer. Each link was classified by destination: hotel or brand website, Google, Tripadvisor, OTA or booking site, Yelp, Reddit or YouTube, or publisher and other site.

The study uses two link measures, labeled the same way throughout.

**Any hotel website link.** A link to a hotel or hotel-brand website anywhere in the answer, either among the citations or in the answer text.

**Hotel website citation.** A link to a hotel or hotel-brand website among the sources the engine attached to the answer.

The gap between them matters. In 169 answers, a hotel website link appeared in the answer text while no hotel website was among the citations. Counting citations alone would understate the hotel links present in the full answer by 18.8 percentage points.

## What this means for a luxury hotel

---

The study measures links, not lost bookings. What follows is interpretation.

**If the traveler just searches the hotel’s name and books direct, what did the hotel lose?** Possibly nothing. One potential risk sits in the pages between the AI answer and the booking. A traveler who follows a link to a Google hotel page, a Tripadvisor listing or a publisher’s list meets rate comparisons, reviews, competing hotels and OTA offers before reaching the hotel. Some of those travelers book direct. Some book elsewhere. Standard last-click analytics credit the final route, not the AI answer that helped form the shortlist. This study does not measure how often that sequence occurs.

In order of effort, a hotel can act on these findings:

**1. Check the Google information.** Observed: Google AI Mode presented hotels in cards inside Google’s interface, and nearly every card held the hotel’s website. Check: the website, phone number, hours and description Google holds for the property. Not tested: how a change to that information affects an AI Mode answer.

**2. Check the Tripadvisor listing.** Observed: Tripadvisor was the recorded provider for 1,472 of 1,550 ChatGPT map pins and received 21.0% of ChatGPT’s citation links. Check: the property name, category, description, amenities and recent reviews. Not tested: whether editing a Tripadvisor listing changes ChatGPT’s answer.

**3. Search for lookalike pages.** Observed: third-party pages built on hotel-named web addresses appeared in ChatGPT’s background search results in 27 answers. Check: search the hotel’s name with words such as hotel, resort and the destination, and look for sites built on the hotel’s name that the hotel does not control.

**4. Measure presence, not only referrals.** Observed: most answers that named a hotel carried no link to a hotel website. Check: whether the hotel appears in AI answers, for which questions, and where those answers link, using a fixed question set and repeated runs. Watch branded search and direct traffic beside the AI referral line, without assuming every change comes from AI.

**5. Know the lists the engines cite.** Observed: ChatGPT and Gemini sent most of their citation links to publishers, guides and ratings bodies. Check: which of those pages cover the hotel’s destination and category. Not tested: whether gaining coverage changes how often an engine names a hotel.

AGR’s [Knowledge Formation Optimization](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/knowledge-formation-optimization-kfo/) work corrects and strengthens the public record AI assistants draw from.

## Methodology

---

**Design.** 100 hotel questions across 10 destinations, 10 questions per destination, asked three times each on ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Gemini. 900 answers. The question set is the same one used in AGR’s September 27, 2026 study. The destinations were New York City, Greater Miami, Las Vegas, Chicago, the Los Angeles area, the Orlando area, Cancún and Costa Mujeres, Punta Cana, Los Cabos and Honolulu.

**Questions.** Eight shared intents per destination: general hotel discovery, luxury, romantic getaway, family vacation, boutique hotels, spas, on-site dining and exceptional service. Two local questions per destination, such as Walt Disney World and water parks in Orlando, and Broadway and Central Park in New York City.

**Capture conditions.** Consumer AI search experiences, logged out, US location, collected November 11, 2026, between 10:33 a.m. and 12:05 p.m. Eastern time. Each question and run produced one saved answer. Collection errors were retried until an answer was saved. No answers were selected or discarded.

**Platform inclusion.** Copilot, part of the September study, could not be collected and is excluded. Claude, Perplexity and other AI assistants were not tested.

**Two link measures.** A hotel website citation is a link to a hotel or hotel-brand website among the sources the engine attached to the answer. Any hotel website link adds hyperlinks to hotel or hotel-brand websites in the answer text, excluding images. Each distinct URL counts once per answer. The saved answers record that these links were present. The study did not verify how each link rendered on screen.

**Classification.** Each link was classified by domain: hotel or brand website, including official subdomains, hotel collections and portfolio sites; Google; Tripadvisor; OTA or booking site, including travel agencies and services that arrange stays; Yelp, Reddit or YouTube; or publisher and other site. Every domain classified as publisher or other was reviewed for official hotel websites.

**Hotels named.** Answers were matched against AGR’s verified hotel list and documented name variants for each destination from the September study. Hotels outside that list are not counted, so “named a hotel” figures are conservative.

**Map pins and hotel cards.** ChatGPT map pins were attributed by the data provider recorded for each pin. Google AI Mode hotel cards were checked for a hotel website in the card’s website field and for the destination of the card’s link field.

**Background sources.** ChatGPT’s full search-result list, which is not shown as citations, was used only for the lookalike-page observation.

**Repeat consistency.** For each engine and question, the hotels named in the three answers were compared. A group counts as changed if any answer named a different set of hotels. One Gemini answer ended after 75 characters and named no hotel. Its group is excluded from the 292 of 299 figure. Including it, 293 of 300 saved groups changed.

**Limits.** One collection day. Results can change when questions are repeated. The study measures hotels named and links present, not clicks, traffic or bookings. A link to a hotel or hotel-brand website was counted at the answer level, not matched to each hotel named. The November Gemini collection recorded no Google Travel cards, so Gemini cannot be compared with the September study on that point. September and November used different collection methods, so any comparison of link shares between them is directional only.

[Does AI Recommend the Same Hotels Five Weeks Later?](https://www.americasgreatresorts.net/ai-hotel-recommendations-five-weeks-later/) found that seven of 10 destinations kept the same leading hotel, while individual recommendation lists frequently changed.

Five hotels per market, on average, captured half of the slots. It used a different question set, markets and protocol, and its data are not pooled with this study.
